# Canonical Authority Twenty-Field Ceiling-Raising Stress Test

Date: 2026-09-21
Workstream: WS:2026-09-21:CANON-20-FIELD-CEILING-SCAN
Project: canonical-authority
Controlling spine: A -> Q -> H[F] -> R -> B -> C

## Purpose

Test whether a fixed argument spine permits broad interdisciplinary exploration
without bloating or replacing the paper.

For each field the scan asks:

1. What transferable distinction or control does the field contribute?
2. What is the smallest spine attachment?
3. Does it strengthen a defensible publication-level result?
4. Does the gain exceed scope, complexity, citation, and reviewer-risk cost?
5. Is the result article-visible, research-control-only, manuscript-only, or rejected?

This is a stress test of the ceiling-raising architecture. It does not assume that
every field must survive into the article.

## Disposition vocabulary

- CENTRAL: materially strengthens the paper's general contribution and can be visible.
- SUPPORT: strengthens a load-bearing distinction but is subordinate.
- RESEARCH_ONLY: useful for discovery/audit but normally invisible in the article.
- MANUSCRIPT_ONLY: improves reader experience or drafting, not the scholarly result.
- REJECT: analogy or complexity cost exceeds publication gain.

## Twenty-field scan

### 1. REBT: ABC separation

Field principle:
REBT separates activating event, belief/evaluation, and emotional/behavioral
consequence rather than attributing the consequence directly to the event.

Transfer:
Keep source event/content, interpretive bridge, and adjudicative result distinct.

Spine attachment: R.

Effect:
Strengthens source-versus-application discipline but adds no new article-level claim
beyond the existing licensed-transfer architecture.

Disposition: RESEARCH_ONLY.

Source anchor:
Recent REBT ABC-model work continues to treat beliefs as mediators between activating
events and consequences.

### 2. REBT: primary versus derivative irrational beliefs

Field principle:
REBT distinguishes load-bearing demandingness from derivative awfulizing,
frustration intolerance, and global evaluation patterns.

Transfer:
Distinguish upstream bridge assumptions from downstream consequences. Do not treat
every discovered implication as an independent paper thesis.

Spine attachment: R -> C.

Effect:
Supports recursive discovery nesting and anti-bloat architecture.

Disposition: RESEARCH_ONLY.

### 3. REBT: global rating versus unconditional acceptance

Field principle:
REBT rejects global rating of a complex person from limited performances or traits.

Transfer:
A global label such as "canonical authority" or "authentic tradition" cannot do
unindexed epistemic work. The relevant property must be decomposed by target,
domain, function, and question.

Spine attachment: Q -> H[F] -> C.

Effect:
Converges strongly with CR-001 target-relative authority, but stronger and more
disciplinarily relevant epistemic-authority literature already supports the point.

Disposition: RESEARCH_ONLY as independent source; convergent support for CR-001.

### 4. CBT: case formulation

Field principle:
CBT case formulation is a revisable, evidence-constrained hypothesis linking
mechanisms to a particular case rather than a mere diagnostic label.

Transfer:
Treat each H-module application as a case-specific reconstruction that can be tested
and revised rather than as a self-applying doctrine.

Spine attachment: H[F] -> R.

Effect:
Improves methodological humility and module discipline, but does not raise the
article's scholarly ceiling by itself.

Disposition: RESEARCH_ONLY.

### 5. ACT: functional contextualism

Field principle:
ACT emphasizes the function of a process in context rather than classifying it by
surface form alone.

Transfer:
Mechanisms with similar religious labels can perform different native functions;
the same label does not license a common epistemic role.

Spine attachment: H[F] -> R.

Effect:
Convergent support for the existing native-function requirement.

Disposition: RESEARCH_ONLY because direct authority/religion literature is a better
article-facing basis.

### 6. DBT: dialectics

Field principle:
DBT works with valid polarities and synthesis without treating every pair of opposed
statements as a single literal descriptive contradiction.

Transfer:
The existence of legitimate opposing perspectives does not establish that
incompatible descriptive propositions are both true. Pluralism and truth evaluation
must remain target-indexed.

Spine attachment: Q -> H[F] -> C.

Effect:
Convergent support for CR-005 indexed pluralism.

Disposition: RESEARCH_ONLY; article use would create unnecessary therapeutic analogy.

### 7. Motivational interviewing: evoked change talk

Field principle:
Observed client language is partly intervention-sensitive; clinician behavior can
evoke the very output later treated as evidence.

Transfer:
Observed institutional or interpretive outputs may partly reflect the selecting or
eliciting procedure used to produce them. Outcome alone does not identify an
independent epistemic generator.

Spine attachment: R -> C.

Effect:
Strengthens CR-006 inverse-problem control and hidden-selector sensitivity.

Disposition: RESEARCH_ONLY.

### 8. Family systems therapy: circular causality and context

Field principle:
Systemic therapy distinguishes circular interactional explanation from simple linear
causality and insists that behavior be interpreted in context.

Transfer:
The paper's source-to-case pathway is an inferential or justificatory pathway, not a
historical causal model of why Rashi or Ramban came to hold their views. Explanation
of causal emergence and assessment of epistemic support are separate graphs.

Spine attachment: Q -> R.

Effect:
Produces a useful robustness clarification that blocks a category error in reading
the arrows of the spine.

Disposition: SUPPORT.

Candidate discovery:
The typed R pathway must be explicitly marked inferential/justificatory rather than
historical-causal.

### 9. Education: cognitive load theory

Field principle:
Reader performance can be impaired by unnecessary processing demands even when the
underlying content is correct.

Transfer:
The research artifact may be large while reader-facing prose hides non-load-bearing
machinery and uses progressive disclosure.

Spine attachment: none to substantive spine; MANUSCRIPT_CONTRACT only.

Effect:
Raises execution quality, not scholarly contribution.

Disposition: MANUSCRIPT_ONLY.

### 10. Education: retrieval practice

Field principle:
Active retrieval can strengthen retention and expose retrieval failure better than
mere restudy.

Transfer:
Repeatedly reconstruct the spine, proposition, and source-module outputs from
controlled records during audits rather than merely rereading summaries.

Spine attachment: research process only.

Effect:
Potentially improves project reliability, not article ceiling.

Disposition: RESEARCH_ONLY.

### 11. Education: worked examples

Field principle:
A worked case can expose the actual sequence of problem solving and support transfer
better than abstract rules alone.

Transfer:
The fixed Rashi-Ramban case can function as a worked demonstration of the
authority-transfer problem. The general contribution emerges from one deeply
controlled case rather than from a broad survey.

Spine attachment: A -> R -> C.

Effect:
Clarifies why retaining one fixed case is a strength rather than a limitation. It
supports the manuscript architecture without requiring expansion to many cases.

Disposition: MANUSCRIPT_ONLY with publication-strategy value.

### 12. Education: formative assessment

Field principle:
Evidence becomes formative when it changes subsequent instructional decisions;
feedback is not useful merely because it exists.

Transfer:
A ceiling scan earns its cost only when it changes a claim, control, source need,
manuscript decision, or future search frontier.

Spine attachment: project control, not substantive spine.

Effect:
Useful governance test for whether future broad scans are still productive.

Disposition: RESEARCH_ONLY.

### 13. Education: transfer of learning

Field principle:
Transfer is not simply present or absent; distance can vary across domain,
functional context, social context, modality, and other dimensions.

Transfer:
"Licensed transfer" in R can be stress-tested across distinct coordinates rather
than treated as one binary bridge. Relevant dimensions for this paper include at
least native domain, native function, target proposition, bearer, evaluator, and
evidential role.

Spine attachment: R -> B.

Effect:
Substantially sharpens the internal transfer test, but educational transfer is an
analogy rather than direct evidence about epistemic authority.

Disposition: SUPPORT as research method; normally invisible as a cited field.

Candidate control:
Represent transfer as a typed vector of coordinates rather than a generic arrow when
a case is sensitive to more than one transfer dimension.

### 14. Jewish education: havruta pedagogy

Field principle:
Havruta theory models text study as a structured relationship involving two learners
and the text, with practices of listening/articulating, wondering/focusing, and
supporting/challenging.

Transfer:
The evaluator is not merely choosing between prestigious persons; the textual object
remains an independent evidential term. Third-party adjudication is at least
evaluator-authority-text relational rather than authority-status-only.

Spine attachment: A -> Q.

Effect:
Provides a useful Jewish-pedagogical reminder to keep the text itself visible as
evidence, but the paper already does this through the fixed grammatical proposition.

Disposition: SUPPORT, normally not article-visible unless the evaluator relation
needs explicit development.

### 15. Jewish education: transmission versus interpretive facilitation

Field principle:
Jewish education research distinguishes transmission pedagogy from interpretive
facilitation and documents how teacher interpretive stance shapes classroom
participation and meaning-making.

Transfer:
"Transmission" and "interpretation" are not interchangeable functions. A later
interpretive procedure cannot be silently attributed to the transmitted content.

Spine attachment: H[F] -> R -> C.

Effect:
This is unusually close to the paper's source/application distinction inside a
Jewish textual setting. It can strengthen Jewish-studies framing, though classroom
pedagogy cannot establish medieval epistemic history.

Disposition: SUPPORT; potential footnote or framing source, not load-bearing evidence.

### 16. Paleontology: taphonomy

Field principle:
The observed fossil record is a filtered record shaped by preservation, transport,
sampling, and publication biases.

Transfer:
The surviving canonical record is not automatically identical to the historical
space of available positions, evidence, or selectors. Survival and prominence are
filters, not direct proof of original epistemic status.

Spine attachment: H[F] -> C.

Effect:
Strengthens the boundedness/open-inventory rule and warns against reverse inference
from survival.

Disposition: RESEARCH_ONLY. A paleontological citation in the paper would be analogy
rather than direct support; use historiography/canon scholarship if this becomes
article-visible.

### 17. Paleontology: phylogenetic systematics and model dependence

Field principle:
Phylogenetic conclusions can depend on character coding, sampling, assumed taxonomy,
and model choices; model trees themselves can bias downstream paleobiological tests.

Transfer:
The chosen taxonomy of "roads" or source modules can itself influence comparative
results. This validates the current decision not to make an exhaustive road identity
theory load-bearing.

Spine attachment: H[F] -> R -> C.

Effect:
Strengthens representation/taxonomy sensitivity and open-inventory discipline.

Disposition: RESEARCH_ONLY.

### 18. Jurisprudence: formal legal truth versus substantive truth

Field principle:
Legal adjudication can legitimately produce a formal or institutional result that
diverges from substantive truth because procedures also serve fairness, finality,
burden allocation, and other values.

Transfer:
A halakhic or institutional mechanism can successfully settle a normative/legal
output without functioning as a procedure for discovering an antecedent descriptive
truth.

Spine attachment: Q -> H[F] -> C.

Effect:
Directly raises the paper's interdisciplinary significance and strengthens the
target-typing result with a genuine neighboring field rather than analogy.

Disposition: CENTRAL SUPPORT.

Article use:
Yes. This is a legitimate cross-disciplinary comparator for the distinction between
institutional settlement and descriptive truth.

### 19. Software engineering: requirements traceability

Field principle:
Traceability links a requirement to its origins and downstream artifacts so later
outputs can be attributed, validated, changed, and impact-analyzed without collapsing
source and implementation.

Transfer:
The paper's source -> bridge -> target chain can be treated as a provenance graph.
If a selector appears only after an added bridge, traceability prevents attribution
of that selector to the source.

Spine attachment: R -> C.

Effect:
Strongly validates the internal attribution architecture and GitHub design.

Disposition: RESEARCH_ONLY. Software-engineering citation would distract in the
article unless the method itself becomes a separate methodological paper.

### 20. Art conservation: authenticity, restoration, and object identity

Field principle:
Conservation scholarship treats authenticity as multidimensional and shows that
material continuity, original appearance, authorial intention, provenance, function,
and later intervention can diverge.

Transfer:
"Authentic" cannot be used as an untyped selector. A claim such as "authentic
mesorah" must specify what dimension of authenticity is meant and why that dimension
bears on the target proposition. Restoration/representation also sharpens bearer
identity.

Spine attachment: A -> Q -> R -> C.

Effect:
Produces a potentially useful general distinction about authenticity labels, but
the paper only benefits if authenticity remains a live term in the manuscript.

Disposition: SUPPORT / CONDITIONAL.

Candidate discovery:
Authenticity is attribute-indexed; authenticity on provenance or continuity does not
by itself entail truth of every proposition carried by the authentic object or
tradition.

## Cross-field result

The twenty-field scan does not justify twenty additions to the paper.

It produces five different output classes:

1. direct article-level interdisciplinary support;
2. subordinate conceptual support;
3. under-the-hood research controls;
4. manuscript-only execution controls;
5. rejected or redundant analogies.

The strongest article-level survivor is jurisprudence because it independently
instantiates the distinction between institutional determination and substantive
truth.

The strongest methodological survivors are:

- inferential pathway versus historical-causal explanation;
- multi-coordinate transfer testing;
- transmission versus interpretive facilitation;
- attribute-indexed authenticity.

The strongest manuscript survivor is:

- the fixed Rashi-Ramban case as a worked demonstration of a general transfer
  problem rather than as a claim to statistical representativeness.

The remaining fields mostly provide convergent audit evidence for controls already
in the spine and ceiling register. Their value is precisely that they can improve
the research without appearing in the article.

## Stress-test verdict

The fixed spine passed the broad-search stress test.

Broad exploration generated useful distinctions without requiring:

- a new spine node;
- a new top-level goal;
- a second paper inside the paper;
- an exhaustive interdisciplinary literature review;
- a change to the fixed Rashi-Ramban case.

The ceiling-raising mechanism also passed a rejection test: most fields did not earn
article visibility.

This indicates that the current architecture can support wide exploratory search
while using the spine and ceiling gate to prevent scope accumulation.
