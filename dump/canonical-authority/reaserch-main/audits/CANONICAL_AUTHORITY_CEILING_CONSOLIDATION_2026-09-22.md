2. its outcome-material weights, priorities, thresholds, dependence assumptions, and

`G_e(tau) = {Delta_i^tau | RouteGround_e(H_i,tau)}`

`K_e(G,tau) = {kappa | Adm_e(kappa | G,tau)}`.

If

`COMPOSE_kappa1(G) != COMPOSE_kappa2(G)`,

the difference in final verdict cannot be attributed to the fixed source-route
grounds alone.

This is the multi-route extension of the project's source-versus-bridge attribution
control.

outcome-material selector.

# II. Three successive post-COMPOSE ceiling rounds

## Round A. Frame-versus-contribution factorization

A prior formulation risked treating every result-sensitive coordinate as the same kind
of selector.

The stronger distinction is:

`Phi = <A, tau, e>`

where:

- A is the declared admitted corpus;
- tau is the fixed target;
- e is the declared evaluator regime.

Phi defines the inquiry frame.

Let

`Psi = <rho, beta, kappa, ...>`

contain outcome-material inferential machinery such as representations, source-to-
target bridges, supplementary premises, thresholds, defeat relations, and composition
rules.

Let S be source-attributable content.

Represent the verdict schematically as:

`V = F(S; Phi, Psi)`.

Changing the target, corpus, or evaluator can define a different question; such
variation is not automatically illicit supplementation.

But when S and Phi are held fixed and materially admissible variation in Psi changes V,
the verdict difference is not attributable to S alone.

This unifies bridge attribution, representation sensitivity, composition sensitivity,
and meta-selector sensitivity without flattening framing coordinates into inferential
contributions.

## Round B. Source-ground versus evaluator-uptake factorization

The current route predicate is evaluator-indexed:

`RouteGround_e(H_i,tau)`.

The route can be factored conceptually into two jobs.

Source-side ground:

`SourceGround(H_i,tau,Delta) = TRACE(H_i,tau,Delta) AND DISCRIMINATE(Delta,tau)`.

Evaluator uptake:

`Usable_e(Delta,tau) = RECOGNIZE_e(Delta,tau)`.

Thus:

`RouteGround_e(H_i,tau)`

holds only when a source-ground exists and evaluator e has warranted grounds to
identify and use it.

This clarifies the etic/emic boundary. A scholar can analyze whether a route is usable
relative to evaluator regime e without personally accepting every premise of e.

It also exposes an important current-case robustness fact: the current admitted routes
fail upstream at TRACE, so evaluator variation cannot rescue the current case unless
new source-to-target content first reaches TRACE.

# III. Maximum-hostility repair

## Counterexample to singleton-only TRACE

Let H_1 supply a target-relevant premise missing from H_2, and H_2 supply the
complementary premise needed to establish a target trace.

It is possible that:

`not TRACE(H_1,tau)`

and

`not TRACE(H_2,tau)`

while

`TRACE({H_1,H_2},tau)`

holds under an independently licensed joint-use relation.

Therefore:

singleton TRACE failure does not by itself prove corpus-level TRACE failure.

## Repair: source-package TRACE

Let `AdmPkg_A` be the set of independently admissible nonempty source packages drawn
from the admitted corpus A.

For X in `AdmPkg_A`, define package-level route success:

`PackageGround_e(X,tau)`

iff there exists Delta such that:

1. TRACE(X,tau,Delta);
2. Delta DISCRIMINATES the fixed rivals concerning tau;
3. evaluator e can RECOGNIZE and use Delta on warranted grounds.

Package TRACE is upstream of COMPOSE.

The distinction is:

- package TRACE allows several source structures jointly to establish one
  target-indexed ground;
- COMPOSE operates when there are several already-established grounds requiring
  aggregation or defeat.

No new reader-facing stage is required.
