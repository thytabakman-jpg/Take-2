## Ceiling test

A field transfer counts as a ceiling-raising candidate only when it:

1. preserves the fixed case, goals, and spine;
2. attaches to a precise spine node or dependency;
3. produces a strict gain in characterization, robustness, generality, explanatory
   compression, independent validation, or publication-level significance;
4. remains defensible without importing an alien field's conclusions as authority;
5. adds more scholarly value than scope, complexity, citation, and reviewer cost;
6. survives regression against the fixed collision, bounded result, positive control,
   open corpus, and no-universal-negative constraints.

An analogy alone does not count.

## Convergent result

The twenty fields do not supply a universal composition rule. They converge on a
stronger result:

> COMPOSE is not one generic aggregation step. It is an independently licensed
> second-order operator whose domain, dependence assumptions, conflict semantics,
> weights or priorities, and stopping behavior can determine the verdict.

That strengthens rather than replaces `CANON:CR:020`.

## Ceiling candidate A: common-target composition domain

### Discovery

The existing ordering of the method can be justified more strongly than the current
architecture states.

For heterogeneous source structures, raw native outputs are not yet guaranteed to be
commensurable. Each source first has to be PROFILEd, TRACEd to the fixed target, and
shown to DISCRIMINATE that target. Only then are the resulting route-level
contributions in a common comparison space.

Therefore COMPOSE normally operates over target-indexed route contributions
`Delta_i^tau`, not over untyped native outputs `o_i`.

### Strict gain

The current architecture places COMPOSE after route-level success. The successor
candidate explains why that placement is load-bearing rather than presentational:
pre-TRACE aggregation would either combine incompatible output types or silently add
a cross-type bridge not licensed by the sources.

### Spine effect

Strengthens `H[F] -> R -> B -> C` without changing any node.

## Ceiling candidate B: dependence-aware composition

### Discovery

Several apparently distinct routes can inherit information from common sources,
shared assumptions, shared institutions, or common interpretive premises.

A COMPOSE rule that treats them as independent can double-count one evidential basis.

### Candidate control

No independence assumption is free. COMPOSE must either:

1. use a rule whose validity does not require route independence; or
2. expose and justify the dependence structure required by the chosen rule.

### Strict gain

This adds a missing anti-inflation control to the general multi-route theory while
leaving the current fixed case untouched.

## Ceiling candidate C: independently licensed weights, priorities, and defeat rules

### Discovery

Once multiple route-level grounds exist, the project can combine them by weighting,
priority, defeat, pooling, or another operator. None of those choices follows merely
from route success.

Where a composition rule contains weights, priorities, thresholds, or calibration
parameters, those parameters require grounds independent of the desired result in the
case being adjudicated.

Target-relevant prospective calibration is one possible licensing basis when the
domain permits it. Tradition-internal priority rules can be another when they are
source-grounded and applicable to the exact target. Prestige or canonical status
alone does not generate an evidential weight.

### Strict gain

This converts the existing phrase "independently licensed, non-outcome-driven" into
an auditable criterion.

## Ceiling candidate D: robustness and sensitivity of case selection

### Discovery

A strict ordering produced by one admissible weighting or aggregation specification
can disappear or reverse under another nearby admissible specification.

### Candidate control

When COMPOSE contains materially contestable weights, dependence assumptions,
thresholds, or priority settings, decisive case selection needs a robustness check
over the admissible specification space.

A verdict that depends on one unlicensed or fragile parameterization is not yet an
all-things-considered selector.

### Strict gain

This extends PD-style result-sensitivity control into the current highest-leverage
Canonical Authority frontier without adding a new manuscript-stage primitive by
default.

## Ceiling candidate E: non-forcing composition

### Discovery

Several fields preserve unresolved or set-valued uncertainty rather than forcing a
single winner.

### Candidate control

COMPOSE must permit at least three result classes:

1. strict warranted ordering of the rivals;
2. warranted tie or partial ordering;
3. unresolved because the available grounds or composition license do not determine
   an ordering.

### Strict gain

This makes the bounded no-selector result a first-class lawful output of the general
method rather than merely the absence of success.

## Successor formal candidate

Let

`G_e(tau) = {Delta_i^tau | RouteGround_e(H_i,tau)}`.

A case-level selector can be represented provisionally as:

`CaseSelect_e(Hset,tau)`

only when there exists a composition specification `kappa` such that:

1. `G_e(tau)` contains only target-compatible route contributions;
2. every dependence assumption material to `kappa` is exposed and warranted;
3. `kappa`, including any weights, priorities, thresholds, or defeat relations, has
   an independently licensed basis;
4. the location of composition in the reasoning chain is licensed rather than
   silently moved upstream across incompatible source types;
5. the resulting strict ordering is robust across the materially admissible
   specifications relevant to the claim being made.

Otherwise the case-level output remains non-selecting or partially ordered.

This is a characterization candidate, not yet a promoted theorem.

## Ceiling effect

The strongest gain is not another interdisciplinary analogy. It is a substantive
successor theory of the existing COMPOSE frontier.

Before this scan:

`COMPOSE = explicit, independently licensed, non-outcome-driven aggregation or defeat`.

After the scan, candidate ceiling:

`COMPOSE = common-target, type-compatible, dependence-aware, independently licensed,
stage-explicit, robustness-tested aggregation/defeat with a lawful non-selecting
output`.

This raises the theoretical ceiling of the general method while preserving the fixed
Jewish case and the current route-level architecture.

It does not by itself establish acceptance probability at any journal, close the
nearest-neighbor literature, or prove that these conditions are jointly minimal.

## PD crossover: COMPOSE as a hidden-selector location

The scan exposes a direct PD attribution result.

Hold the route-ground set `G_e(tau)` fixed. Let two admissible-looking composition
specifications `kappa_1` and `kappa_2` produce different strict orderings of the same
rivals.

Then the difference in case-level output is not attributable to the fixed source
routes alone. It is result-sensitive to the composition specification.

Formally, when:

`G_e(tau)` is fixed,

but

`COMPOSE_kappa1(G_e(tau)) != COMPOSE_kappa2(G_e(tau))`,

the composition layer is a material selector.

Therefore any case-level verdict must distinguish at least:

1. discriminatory information supplied by the source routes; and
2. selecting information supplied by the composition rule, including weights,
   priorities, thresholds, dependence assumptions, and defeat semantics.

A source-level authority claim cannot inherit the selector contribution of an
analyst-supplied COMPOSE rule by attributional collapse. This is the multi-route
analogue of the project's existing source-versus-bridge control.

This result materially links the current Canonical Authority frontier to PD hidden-
selector and result-sensitivity work without making PD an article-visible second
framework by default.

## Promotion recommendation

The strongest promotable package is a refinement of `CANON:CR:020`, not a new paper
identity:

1. fix COMPOSE's domain at common-target route contributions;
2. add dependence/no-double-counting control;
3. operationalize independent licensing of weights/priorities/defeat rules;
4. add conditional robustness testing;
5. explicitly permit non-selecting outputs.

Because the active backend-recovery workstream overlaps all Canonical Authority
objects, this audit does not promote those changes yet.
