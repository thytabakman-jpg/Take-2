# Canonical Authority COMPOSE Hostile Minimality Audit

## Objective

Attack the current COMPOSE frontier until either it collapses, exposes a missing
primitive, or yields a smaller defensible characterization.

Protected spine:

`A -> Q -> H[F] -> R -> B -> C`

Protected route-level method:

typed setup -> TRACE -> DISCRIMINATE -> RECOGNIZE.

No test in this audit changes the fixed Rashi-Ramban case or the current bounded
no-selector result.

## Baseline

Current architecture:

`CaseSelect_e(Hset,tau)` iff a separately licensed `COMPOSE_A` over successful
route-level contributions yields a strict warranted ordering of the rivals.

Problem: an existential formulation over composition rules is vulnerable to hidden
selector injection. With the route grounds held fixed, an analyst can choose a
composition rule that creates the desired result.

## Hostile cases

### H1 Arbitrary-weight reversal

Hold two successful route grounds fixed:

- Delta_1 supports P_R;
- Delta_2 supports P_N.

Under equal weights the result ties.
Under weights 0.9/0.1 P_R wins.
Under weights 0.1/0.9 P_N wins.

Result: route success does not determine case selection. Material weights are part of
the selector and require independent grounds.

Verdict: independent licensing is necessary.

### H2 Duplicate-support inflation

Let H_1 and H_2 appear to be distinct authority routes but inherit the same decisive
premise/source, while H_3 is genuinely independent and points the other way.

A naive vote counts 2-to-1 and selects the duplicated side.

Result: route multiplicity is not evidential independence.

Verdict: dependence cannot be ignored where the selected composition rule is
dependence-sensitive. Dependence treatment belongs inside the licensed composition
specification rather than as a universal free-standing stage.

### H3 Pre-TRACE type mixing

Combine one route whose native output is legal settlement with another whose native
output is proposition-specific evidence before either is typed to the common truth
target.

Result: the aggregation creates an implicit bridge between output types.

Verdict: type compatibility and stage location are load-bearing, but they are already
enforced by defining COMPOSE's domain over successful target-indexed route grounds.
They are not independent COMPOSE primitives.

### H4 Unique licensed priority rule

Suppose the relevant tradition itself supplies a clear target-applicable priority rule
between two route classes, the rule is independently source-grounded, and no competing
admissible priority rule survives.

Result: a strict case ordering can be licensed without a separate sensitivity sweep
over invented alternatives.

Verdict: robustness testing is not universally necessary. It becomes necessary for an
unqualified verdict when multiple materially admissible composition specifications
remain.

### H5 Multiple admissible rules, opposite winners

Suppose kappa_1 and kappa_2 are each independently defensible and neither has a
further licensed priority over the other.

- kappa_1 selects P_R;
- kappa_2 selects P_N.

Result: an existential rule would permit either conclusion.

Verdict: an unqualified all-things-considered selector is unavailable. Only
kappa-relative results are warranted unless an additional licensed meta-relation
removes the underdetermination.

### H6 Forced determinacy

All successful route grounds remain balanced or incomparable.

A method is defined to always output exactly one winner.

Result: the method's forced output contains selecting information not supplied by the
grounds.

Verdict: the output space must permit tie, partial ordering, or unresolved status.
Non-forcing is a codomain/integrity requirement, not an additional success conjunct.

## Deletion and collapse tests

### Candidate: common-target/type-compatible composition

Deletion causes invalid cross-type aggregation.

But once the input set is defined as successful target-indexed route grounds, the
condition is guaranteed by construction.

Disposition: collapse into COMPOSE domain definition and upstream TRACE.

### Candidate: dependence-aware composition

Deletion can produce double counting in dependence-sensitive rules.

But some composition rules do not require independence.

Disposition: retain as a material admissibility obligation inside kappa when relevant,
not a universal stage.

### Candidate: independently licensed weights/priorities/defeat

Deletion permits arbitrary outcome manufacturing.

Disposition: survives as the core COMPOSE constraint.

### Candidate: stage-explicit composition

Deletion can smuggle aggregation upstream across incompatible source outputs.

But this is prevented by the domain restriction to successful route grounds.

Disposition: collapse into domain definition and source-to-target architecture.

### Candidate: robustness across composition specifications

Deletion permits an arbitrary choice among multiple admissible kappas.

But where exactly one kappa is independently admissible, no alternative-specification
sensitivity remains.

Disposition: conditional necessity for unqualified case selection when
`|K_e(G,tau)| > 1`.

### Candidate: non-forcing output

Deletion can manufacture a winner from genuine underdetermination.

Disposition: retain as an output-space integrity condition, not a success conjunct.

## Successor representation

Let

`G_e(tau) = {Delta_i^tau | RouteGround_e(H_i,tau)}`

be the set of evaluator-usable target-indexed route grounds.

Let `Adm_e(kappa | G_e(tau), tau)` mean that composition specification `kappa` is
independently licensed for this target and input set. Its material weights, priorities,
thresholds, defeat relations, and dependence assumptions must have grounds independent
of the desired case result.

Define:

`K_e(G,tau) = {kappa | Adm_e(kappa | G,tau)}`.

### Kappa-relative selection

`Select_e^kappa(P_j | G,tau)`

holds when an admissible `kappa` applied to `G` yields a strict warranted ordering
of `P_j` over its rival.

This states what follows relative to a particular licensed composition specification.

### Robust case selection

`RobustSelect_e(P_j | G,tau)`

holds when:

1. `K_e(G,tau)` is nonempty; and
2. every materially admissible `kappa in K_e(G,tau)` yields the same strict
   warranted ordering with `P_j` above its rival.

Where exactly one kappa is admissible, condition 2 is trivially satisfied.

Where admissible specifications disagree, the method returns no unqualified strict
selector unless a further independently licensed meta-relation narrows K.

## Hidden-selector attribution result

Hold `G_e(tau)` fixed.

If two admissible or apparently admissible composition specifications produce
different outputs, then the case-level result is composition-sensitive:

`COMPOSE_kappa1(G) != COMPOSE_kappa2(G)`.

The difference cannot be attributed to the source routes alone because their
contributions were held fixed.

Therefore the final verdict has at least two possible sources of selecting
information:

1. source-route discriminatory content;
2. composition-specification content.

Any claim that "the authorities select P_j" must exclude material selector contribution
introduced only by kappa or state that contribution explicitly.

This is the multi-route form of the project's source-versus-bridge attribution
control.

## Minimality result

The five-field-derived COMPOSE proposal compresses.

The smallest surviving reader-level structure is:

1. COMPOSE operates only on successful target-indexed route grounds;
2. the composition specification is independently licensed;
3. unqualified selection is invariant across the materially admissible specification
   set;
4. the output space permits non-selection.

Dependence models, weights, thresholds, priorities, defeat relations, and stage
location remain diagnostic contents of domain/admissibility rather than new
reader-facing stages.

## Non-regression

PASS.

The fixed case, positive control, source-module results, open corpus, no-universal-
negative constraint, and TRACE/DISCRIMINATE/RECOGNIZE architecture all survive.

## Ceiling effect

This is stronger and simpler than the pre-audit COMPOSE proposal.

The previous ceiling identified the need for a composition rule.
The successor candidate distinguishes:

- route-relative grounds;
- kappa-relative selection;
- robust all-things-considered selection;
- lawful non-selection;
- selector attribution.

That is a strict theoretical gain on the existing spine.
