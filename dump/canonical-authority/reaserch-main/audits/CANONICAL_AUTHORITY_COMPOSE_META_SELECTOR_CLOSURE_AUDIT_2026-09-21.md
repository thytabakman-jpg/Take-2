# Canonical Authority COMPOSE Meta-Selector Closure Audit

## Objective

Test whether the strengthened COMPOSE theory merely moves the hidden-selector problem
one level upward when a rule is needed to select among composition rules.

Protected spine and current case remain unchanged.

## Setup

Let:

- G be the fixed set of successful target-indexed route grounds;
- kappa be a composition specification over G;
- K be the materially admissible set of kappas;
- mu be any higher-order rule used to narrow or select within K.

The prior hostile audit established:

- kappa-relative selection can be reported relative to one independently licensed
  kappa;
- an unqualified selection requires invariance across materially admissible kappas or
  a further independently licensed basis for narrowing K.

Question:

Does adding mu generate an infinite regress of meta-composition rules?

## Meta-hostile cases

### M1 Tradition-supplied composition rule

Suppose the religious source tradition itself explicitly states a priority relation
between two authority types that applies to the exact target domain.

Result:

The composition rule is not automatically evaluator-added. It can itself be a
source-grounded object.

Required analysis:

PROFILE/TRACE applies to kappa's provenance and scope just as it applies to other
source material. The claim becomes:

"the tradition supplies both these route grounds and this priority relation"

rather than:

"the route grounds alone entail the verdict."

Verdict:

The theory permits source-attributable composition. Selector attribution is about
provenance, not an assumption that every selector is external.

### M2 Evaluator-supplied but independently justified standard

Suppose the evaluator declares and independently defends a standard for combining
grounds—for example a domain-appropriate evidential rule not supplied by the
religious sources.

Result:

A verdict can be warranted relative to that evaluator standard.

Attribution limit:

The resulting verdict cannot be described without qualification as supplied by the
religious authorities alone. Its grounds have mixed provenance:

- source-route contribution;
- evaluator-level composition contribution.

Verdict:

Mixed provenance is legitimate when explicit. The framework does not require all
selection machinery to come from the religious tradition.

### M3 Meta-rule selects among admissible kappas

Suppose kappa_1 and kappa_2 remain materially admissible and yield opposite winners.
The evaluator introduces mu to select kappa_1.

Hold G and K fixed.

If changing mu changes the final rival ordering, mu is outcome-material.

Result:

The hidden-selector test recurs at the meta-level.

Verdict:

A meta-rule does not escape attribution control merely because it is one level
higher.

### M4 Disputed dependence model

Suppose the weight of two route grounds depends on whether they count as independent.
Two dependence models remain materially defensible and generate different case
orderings.

Result:

The dependence model is an outcome-material selector coordinate.

Verdict:

Either the dependence model receives independent grounds, the verdict is indexed to
that model, or the unqualified result remains unresolved.

No separate universal "dependence stage" is required.

### M5 Partial rather than total composition

Suppose every admissible composition rule agrees that P_R is at least as well
supported as P_N, but some leave them tied and others strictly prefer P_R.

Result:

The robust output is a partial/weak relation rather than a strict selector.

Verdict:

The method can report the common information without forcing stronger determinacy.

### M6 Infinite meta-rule chain

Suppose every proposed selector rule is challenged by another material alternative
and a new meta-rule is introduced solely to choose among the previous alternatives.

Result:

There is no principled reason to force the chain to terminate in a strict winner.

The non-forcing output rule stops the epistemic regress:

when unresolved outcome-material selector variation remains, the strongest warranted
result is indexed, partial, or unresolved.

The theory therefore does not need a uniquely neutral ultimate composition rule.

## Selector-provenance closure candidate

A case-level attribution claim is closed only when every outcome-material selector in
the inferential chain receives one of three dispositions:

1. SOURCE-ATTRIBUTABLE:
   the selector itself is traceable to the relevant source/tradition and applicable
   to the fixed target;

2. EVALUATOR-LICENSED:
   the selector is supplied by an explicitly declared evaluator standard with
   independent grounds, and the final claim is indexed to that contribution where
   attribution requires it;

3. INVARIANT:
   materially admissible alternatives exist, but varying them does not change the
   case-level conclusion at the strength being asserted.

If an outcome-material selector has none of these dispositions, an unqualified strict
case selector is not established.

## Formal sensitivity representation

Let the full case-output function be represented schematically as:

`V = F(G, theta)`

where G is the fixed source-route ground set and theta contains every material
composition, priority, weight, threshold, dependence, or meta-selection coordinate.

A coordinate theta_j is outcome-material for claim q when there exist materially
admissible theta_j and theta_j' such that, holding G and all other relevant coordinates
fixed:

`F(G, theta_j) != F(G, theta_j')`

at the level of q.

For an attribution claim that the religious sources themselves select P_j, every
outcome-material theta_j must either be source-attributable or irrelevant by
invariance. Evaluator-licensed but outcome-material theta_j produces a mixed-provenance
verdict rather than a source-only verdict.

## Strict gain over prior COMPOSE candidate

The prior candidate controlled admissible composition specifications.

This audit adds a stopping and attribution rule for higher-order selector choice.

It shows that the framework need not posit a final neutral meta-rule.

Instead:

- expose outcome-material selector coordinates;
- trace their provenance/license;
- index the claim where evaluator contribution remains;
- preserve partial or unresolved output when selector variation is not closed.

## Regression

PASS.

No current Jewish source-module result changes.

The Rashi-Ramban case remains non-selecting before COMPOSE is activated because no
admitted route yet reaches a complete usable discriminator.

The positive control remains possible.

No goal or spine change is required.

## Publication-value effect

This result raises explanatory power and attack resistance.

A foreseeable reviewer objection is:

"Your requirement that COMPOSE be independently licensed simply moves the question to
how that license is chosen."

The answer is now explicit:

license is itself subject to provenance/result-sensitivity control, and the method
does not force a verdict when outcome-material selector variation remains.

The broader methodological contribution can therefore be stated as an attribution
principle rather than as a claim to possess one universally correct aggregation rule.
