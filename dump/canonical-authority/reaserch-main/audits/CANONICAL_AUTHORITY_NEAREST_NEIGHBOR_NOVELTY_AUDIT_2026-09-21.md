# Canonical Authority Nearest-Neighbor Novelty Audit

## Scope

Targeted review of the closest currently identified intellectual neighbors to the
current Canonical Authority architecture.

This is not an exhaustive priority search. The novelty claim remains bounded to the
reviewed comparator corpus.

## Current candidate contribution

The paper's current general architecture asks a third-party evaluator to compare
heterogeneous religious authority/knowledge structures against one fixed
truth-directed target by:

1. reconstructing each structure in its native function;
2. TRACE: establishing a licensed/source-attributable route to the fixed target;
3. DISCRIMINATE: testing whether the route asymmetrically supports the rivals;
4. RECOGNIZE: testing whether the evaluator has warranted grounds to identify and use
   that discriminator;
5. COMPOSE: when multiple route-level grounds succeed, applying a separately licensed
   case-level composition/defeat relation rather than treating route success as an
   automatic all-things-considered verdict.

The current ceiling candidate further distinguishes kappa-relative selection from
robust selection across the set of materially admissible composition specifications.

## Comparator families

### 1. Joshua Halberstam, epistemic disagreement and elu ve-elu

Closest shared problem:
third-party warrant for choosing between disagreeing superiors in a Talmudic/Jewish
context.

Difference:
the paper does not provide a general typed source-to-target route architecture or a
multi-route composition theory. Its central problem strongly validates Q but does not
replace R/B/COMPOSE.

Novelty role:
closest Jewish-philosophical problem predecessor currently located.

### 2. Religious-disagreement literature

Representative:
Katherine Dormandy; Benton and Kvanvig; James Kraft; contemporary religious
epistemology surveys.

Closest shared problem:
what epistemic effect disagreement has on belief, especially where apparent epistemic
parity exists.

Difference:
the primary unit is typically the believer/disputant and the rational response to
peer or worldview disagreement. The Canonical project instead fixes two historical
canonical claims and asks what selected second-order source structures give a later
third party proposition-specific grounds.

Novelty role:
neighboring epistemic problem, not a duplicate method.

### 3. General expert and epistemic authority

Representative:
Alvin Goldman, *Experts: Which Ones Should You Trust?*;
Linda Zagzebski, *Epistemic Authority*.

Closest shared problem:
grounds for non-experts to defer to or identify epistemic authorities.

Difference:
these accounts theorize expertise/deference broadly. They do not reconstruct
heterogeneous religious mechanisms in native domains and require a source-attributable
target trace, fixed-rival discrimination, and separate composition of multiple
successful authority routes.

Novelty role:
RECOGNIZE and authority-deference neighbor.

### 4. Religious authority as epistemic authority

Representative:
Ali-Reza Bhojani and Morgan Clarke, "Religious Authority beyond Domination and
Discipline" (2023).

Closest shared result:
religious authority is not reducible to domination; practical and epistemic authority
must be distinguished, and authority has diverse uses.

Difference:
their project is ethnographic/conceptual clarification of religious authority in a
Shi'i setting. It does not offer a fixed-proposition third-party adjudication
characterization.

Novelty role:
strong disciplinary support for functionally differentiated religious authority.

### 5. Jewish authority and obedience

Representative:
Avi Sagi, "Models of Authority and the Duty of Obedience in Halakhic Literature."

Closest shared result:
halakhic authority, obedience, social order, and traditional authority are distinct
analytical questions.

Difference:
the Canonical project asks what authority contributes to truth-directed assessment of
a fixed non-halakhic descriptive disagreement and explicitly separates settlement from
truth selection.

Novelty role:
authority/settlement neighbor.

### 6. Rabbinic pluralism and polysemy

Representative:
Avi Sagi; Steven Fraade; Azzan Yadin-Israel.

Closest shared material:
elu ve-elu, multivocality, legitimacy, plurality, and determination.

Difference:
this literature characterizes pluralism, polysemy, or halakhic validity. The current
project treats those as native outputs and asks whether any licensed proposition-
specific transfer makes them truth-discriminating for the fixed Genesis 1:1 rivals.

Novelty role:
H_P literature and anti-equivocation control.

### 7. Epistemic value of religious tradition

Representative:
Matthias Kramm, "Four Epistemic Reasons to Consult Religious Traditions."

Closest shared result:
tradition can supply pro tanto epistemic reasons, and transmission/test-of-time/
intergenerational-learning claims require explicit justification.

Difference:
Kramm addresses reasons to consult traditions for problem-solving generally. The
Canonical project tests a particular tradition-internal source structure against an
exact rival proposition and separates consultation value from adjudicative selection.

Novelty role:
TRACE/tradition neighbor.

### 8. Comparative method in religious studies

Representative:
Oliver Freiberger, *Considering Comparison*.

Closest shared method:
context-sensitive comparison, explicit comparative operations, second-order
metalanguage, and concern about decontextualization/essentialization.

Difference:
Freiberger supplies a general comparative methodology rather than a truth-target
authority-transfer architecture. The Canonical method can be positioned as a
specialized fixed-target comparison inside that broader methodological space.

Novelty role:
disciplinary method predecessor.

### 9. Judgment aggregation and belief merging

Representative:
List/Pettit tradition; contemporary judgment-aggregation literature.

Closest shared problem:
how individually coherent inputs can be transformed into a collective/resultant
judgment, including impossibility and procedure-dependence results.

Difference:
the inputs are normally individual judgment sets, not heterogeneous religious
authority routes whose outputs first require native typing and licensed transfer to a
common truth target.

Novelty role:
closest formal neighbor for COMPOSE.

### 10. Structured expert aggregation and robustness

Representative:
Cooke Classical Model; MCDA sensitivity literature.

Closest shared problem:
weights and aggregation rules require validation; rankings can change under weighting
assumptions.

Difference:
the Canonical project does not assume quantitative expert forecasts or utility
criteria. It uses these fields only to expose the composition-license and
result-sensitivity problem.

Novelty role:
independent validation of selector-attribution and robustness controls.


### 11. Robust ordinal regression

Representative:
Salvatore Greco, Vincent Mousseau, and Roman Słowiński / UTA-GMS robust ordinal
regression literature.

Closest formal pattern:
distinguish a possible preference, supported by at least one compatible value
function, from a necessary preference, supported across all compatible value
functions.

Difference:
the Canonical project does not claim novelty for the quantifier pattern
"one admissible model" versus "all admissible models." Its contribution is to use a
related robustness distinction to control case-level composition of heterogeneous
religious-authority grounds after source-to-target transfer.

Novelty consequence:
do not present robust selection across admissible kappas as a new general decision
theory. Present it as a defensible composition control specialized to the authority
architecture.

### 12. Abstract argumentation: credulous versus skeptical acceptance

Representative:
Dung-style preferred-semantics literature and later algorithms for skeptical
acceptance.

Closest formal pattern:
an argument can be accepted in at least one admissible/preferred extension or in all
preferred extensions.

Difference:
the Canonical project is not choosing extensions of one attack graph. Its admissible
objects are independently licensed composition specifications over already successful
source-grounded route contributions.

Novelty consequence:
the existential-versus-robust distinction has established formal analogues. The
project's more distinctive result is attributional: changing the composition
specification while holding route grounds fixed isolates selector information supplied
by the evaluator's composition layer rather than by the authorities.

## Bounded novelty result

No direct predecessor was located in this targeted review that combines all of the
following in one fixed-target religious-authority method. The project does not claim
novelty for the generic possible/necessary or credulous/skeptical quantifier pattern:

1. heterogeneous religious/Jewish authority structures preserved in native function;
2. source-attributable TRACE to one fixed truth-apt target;
3. rival-specific DISCRIMINATE;
4. evaluator-centered RECOGNIZE;
5. explicit separation of route-level grounds from case-level selection;
6. separately licensed COMPOSE/defeat;
7. attribution control for selector information introduced by the composition rule.

The strongest novelty claim is therefore an intersection claim, not a claim that the
paper independently invented each component.

## Publication-value consequence

This is stronger than saying "no one has written about this."

The defensible contribution is:

existing literatures solve neighboring subproblems, while the Canonical Authority
project integrates the subproblems around one source-grounded fixed truth target and
exposes the missing composition/attribution layer between successful authority routes
and an all-things-considered verdict.
