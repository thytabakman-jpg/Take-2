# Canonical Authority Religion-Field Twenty-Approach Ceiling-Raising Scan

Date: 2026-09-21
Workstream: WS:2026-09-21:CANON-RELIGION-20-FIELD-CEILING-SCAN
Project: canonical-authority
Controlling spine: A -> Q -> H[F] -> R -> B -> C

## Purpose

Test twenty distinct approaches within the academic study of religion against the
fixed Canonical Authority spine.

The task is not to import twenty literatures into the manuscript. It is to ask
whether each field supplies a distinction, method, comparator, or scope control that
raises the strongest defensible contribution while preserving the paper's identity.

Special attention is given to the journal Religion, whose current scope encourages
theoretical and methodological work in the comparative and interdisciplinary study
of religion while excluding straight theology and philosophy of religion.

## Disposition vocabulary

- CENTRAL: changes the strongest article-level contribution or journal fit.
- CENTRAL_SUPPORT: directly strengthens a central contribution but need not control the paper.
- SUPPORT: useful article-facing support or limitation.
- RESEARCH_ONLY: improves audit/research control but normally remains invisible.
- MANUSCRIPT_ONLY: improves reader-facing architecture but not the scholarly result.
- REJECT: insufficient net gain for this paper.

## Twenty approaches

### 1. Sociology of religious knowledge

Core field move:
Treat religions as social formations organized in part around claims about knowledge,
including the production, transmission, authorization, and use of knowledge.

Transfer:
Reframe H[F] not merely as a list of "authority mechanisms" but as distinct
religious knowledge structures whose native functions differ.

Spine attachment: Q -> H[F] -> R -> C.

Ceiling effect:
Raises the project from a philosophy-of-religion-adjacent authority problem into a
live religious-studies conversation about how religions organize knowledge.

New contribution candidate:
A study of religious authority needs to distinguish the social production and
transmission of knowledge from proposition-specific grounds for accepting one claim
over a rival claim.

Disposition: CENTRAL.

### 2. Philosophy of religious studies / metatheory

Core field move:
Use philosophical tools for conceptual and methodological self-critique inside the
academic study of religion without turning the project into philosophy of religion.

Transfer:
Present the paper as a metatheoretical analysis of how scholars and traditions use
the category "religious authority" and what inferential work that category can
legitimately perform.

Spine attachment: Q -> R -> C.

Ceiling effect:
Directly addresses the main journal-fit problem. The paper can use conceptual
analysis while remaining an empirically grounded study of religious materials and
religious-studies method.

Disposition: CENTRAL.

### 3. Comparative method in religious studies

Core field move:
Comparison requires explicit comparands, scale, scope, and tertium comparationis,
with controls against decontextualization and essentialization.

Transfer:
The fixed Rashi-Ramban dispute supplies one explicit target against which
heterogeneous authority structures can be compared without flattening their native
functions.

Spine attachment: A -> H[F] -> R -> C.

Ceiling effect:
Generates a methodological contribution: fixed-target comparison.

Candidate formulation:
Hold one truth-apt religious claim-pair fixed; reconstruct unlike religious
mechanisms in native context; compare only what each contributes to that target.

Disposition: CENTRAL.

### 4. Religious authority studies

Core field move:
"Religious authority" is not one self-explanatory property. Different contexts
instantiate different kinds and uses of authority.

Transfer:
The paper adds an additional analytical layer: identify not only the type of
authority but its native output, target, transfer relation, and third-party
recognizability.

Spine attachment: Q -> H[F] -> R -> B -> C.

Ceiling effect:
Strengthens CR-001 target-relative authority and situates it directly within
comparative religious studies.

Disposition: CENTRAL.

### 5. Sociology of religion: legitimacy, charisma, and routinization

Core field move:
Authority is a social relation whose legitimacy, form, and institutional embodiment
can change historically.

Transfer:
Separate sociological authority-success from epistemic success. A figure can acquire
or lose social authority without that fact alone establishing the truth of a target
proposition.

Spine attachment: H[F] -> R -> C.

Ceiling effect:
Supplies a social-scientific neighboring account of authority and prevents the paper
from treating authority as only an abstract epistemic property.

Disposition: CENTRAL_SUPPORT.

### 6. Scripturalism

Core field move:
Scriptural authority is a community-making configuration of beliefs and practices.
Attribution of ultimate authority to a text can coexist with extensive adaptation
and contestation over meaning.

Transfer:
"Scripture is authoritative" and "this proposition is the uniquely correct meaning
of scripture" are distinct claims.

Spine attachment: A -> H[F] -> C.

Ceiling effect:
Shows within religious-studies theory why high scriptural authority does not
automatically collapse interpretive plurality into proposition-specific truth.

Disposition: CENTRAL_SUPPORT.

### 7. Scripture as socially enacted external authority

Core field move:
Scripture can function as external authority through boundaries, practices,
memorization, interpretation, and community-specific modes of expertise.

Transfer:
Authority recognition is mediated by a bearer, community, practice, and interpreter.
This strengthens W in B**.

Spine attachment: A -> Q -> B.

Ceiling effect:
Gives the third-party warrant layer a social-religious dimension: an authority
relation must be identifiable and usable by an evaluator, not merely posited.

Disposition: SUPPORT.

### 8. Tradition studies

Core field move:
Tradition constrains religious actors while also being reproduced, selected,
expanded, and transformed. It is not a static container.

Transfer:
"Mesorah" or "tradition" cannot be treated as a single undifferentiated epistemic
object whose mere continuity supplies one uniform truth relation.

Spine attachment: H[F] -> R -> C.

Ceiling effect:
Strengthens the anti-reification result and gives it a native religious-studies home.

Disposition: CENTRAL_SUPPORT.

### 9. Reception history

Core field move:
Reception history distinguishes a source from the history of its interpretation,
use, performance, and effects.

Transfer:
Keep Genesis 1:1, Rashi, Ramban, later reports of their positions, later canonical
status, and present analytic reconstruction as distinct historical layers.

Spine attachment: A -> R.

Ceiling effect:
Strengthens source/application and historical-layer control; helps prevent later
reception from being projected backward as original epistemic content.

Disposition: SUPPORT.

### 10. Textual criticism and paratext studies

Core field move:
Texts are transmitted through material witnesses, editions, paratexts, headings,
glosses, critical apparatuses, and other features that can constrain interpretation.

Transfer:
Bearer identity becomes part of the case architecture rather than a minor technical
issue. Reem, Onkelos, vocalization, printed Rashi, and reported Rashi are not one
undifferentiated evidential bearer.

Spine attachment: A -> R.

Ceiling effect:
Strengthens CR-004 bearer/representation control with direct biblical-studies
literature.

Disposition: SUPPORT.

### 11. Cultural memory and canon/archive studies

Core field move:
Canon and archive perform different cultural-memory functions. Canon is active,
circulating memory tied to identity; archive is stored material that can be
reactivated.

Transfer:
Canonical survival or status can be evidence of identity, circulation, or memory
function without thereby being evidence of proposition-level truth.

Spine attachment: H[F] -> R -> C.

Ceiling effect:
Produces a stronger religious-studies account of why canonicity and truth must not
be collapsed.

Candidate formulation:
Canonicality is itself a functional status that requires a separate bridge before
it can serve as a truth selector.

Disposition: CENTRAL_SUPPORT.

### 12. Hermeneutics

Core field move:
Interpretation involves historically situated relations among text, tradition,
reader, meaning, and claims to truth.

Transfer:
Clarifies that the paper is not reducing interpretation to one factual operation.
It isolates one truth-apt coordinate within a larger hermeneutical practice.

Spine attachment: A -> Q -> R.

Ceiling effect:
Prevents overclaiming that the framework is a total theory of interpretation.

Disposition: SUPPORT.

### 13. Translation studies in religion

Core field move:
Translation is not neutral duplication; it involves choices of representation,
equivalence, context, and interpretation.

Transfer:
Onkelos and other translations must be typed as interpretive evidence rather than
treated as transparent copies of the source proposition.

Spine attachment: A -> R.

Ceiling effect:
Sharpens first-order evidence control but does not change the central result.

Disposition: SUPPORT.

### 14. Lived religion

Core field move:
Official or institutionally prescribed religion can differ substantially from
religion as practiced and experienced by actual people.

Transfer:
The paper analyzes canonical textual and institutional resources. It does not claim
that these exhaust how Jews in any historical or contemporary community actually
reason, defer, interpret, or adjudicate.

Spine attachment: H[F] -> C.

Ceiling effect:
Important scope control for Religion. It prevents the paper from presenting textual
elite discourse as Judaism simpliciter.

Disposition: SUPPORT.

### 15. Material religion

Core field move:
Religious meaning and authority are mediated through material forms, bodies, media,
objects, and sensory practices rather than existing only as abstract beliefs.

Transfer:
Textual authority reaches interpreters through material bearers and practices.
This supports bearer/representation control and limits purely propositional
descriptions of religious authority.

Spine attachment: A -> H[F] -> R.

Ceiling effect:
Broadens the religious-studies framing but is not load-bearing for this text-centered
case.

Disposition: SUPPORT.

### 16. Ritual studies and ritualization

Core field move:
Analytical categories such as "ritual" are not always natural kinds waiting to be
found; scholarly classification can reify heterogeneous practices.

Transfer:
"Road," "mesorah," "authority," and even the H-module taxonomy must remain analytic
constructions unless source evidence establishes native unity.

Spine attachment: H[F] -> R.

Ceiling effect:
Strong audit protection against reification; weak manuscript payoff.

Disposition: RESEARCH_ONLY.

### 17. Practice theory / religious field

Core field move:
Religious authority and meaning operate in fields structured by practices,
positions, dispositions, and symbolic resources.

Transfer:
An authority relation is partly relational and situated; it cannot be inferred from
a title or status independent of the practices and field that make it operative.

Spine attachment: H[F] -> B.

Ceiling effect:
Converges with the relational W component but would require substantial sociological
development to become article-central.

Disposition: RESEARCH_ONLY / SUPPORT if needed.

### 18. Discourse and power

Core field move:
Religious discourse can persuade, classify, authorize, constitute identities, and
organize action.

Transfer:
Discursive efficacy is another output type that must not be silently converted into
truth. A religious discourse can be socially powerful even when its epistemic force
on I_S remains unestablished.

Spine attachment: Q -> H[F] -> C.

Ceiling effect:
Strengthens target typing inside religious studies and prevents power/effectiveness
from being conflated with truth.

Disposition: CENTRAL_SUPPORT.

### 19. Critical religion / genealogy / category construction

Core field move:
Scholarly categories have histories and can import assumptions, boundaries, and
power relations into the object of study.

Transfer:
"Religious authority" and the module taxonomy are second-order analytic devices.
Their utility depends on explicit definition and traceability, not on assuming that
the tradition itself contains those categories in the same form.

Spine attachment: H[F] -> R -> C.

Ceiling effect:
Strengthens reflexivity and protects the paper from reifying its own metalanguage.

Disposition: RESEARCH_ONLY with brief methodological visibility if needed.

### 20. Normativity in religious studies

Core field move:
Religious studies must distinguish description and analysis of normative or truth
claims from the scholar's own normative or theological endorsement.

Transfer:
The paper can analyze what internal Jewish structures contribute to adjudicating
P_R/P_N without itself deciding which religious interpretation is true or prescribing
what Jews ought to believe.

Spine attachment: Q -> B -> C.

Ceiling effect:
Directly improves Religion fit by converting a potential philosophy-of-religion
reading into an explicitly second-order analysis of religious knowledge and
authority.

Disposition: CENTRAL.

## Strongest survivors

The scan identifies six central ceiling-raising moves.

### CR-A: Religious knowledge structures

Use the emerging sociology of religious knowledge to frame H[F] as a set of
different structures for producing, transmitting, classifying, authenticating, or
authorizing religious knowledge.

This does not replace "authority" with a new universal term. It supplies a broader
religious-studies conversation in which the paper's target-typing result matters.

### CR-B: Fixed-target comparison

Treat the method as a controlled comparison in which one target proposition-pair is
held fixed while heterogeneous mechanisms are reconstructed in native context.

This gives the paper a comparative-method contribution without requiring multiple
religions or multiple first-order cases.

### CR-C: Metatheory of religious authority

The paper can be positioned as philosophy of religious studies rather than
philosophy of religion: a conceptual and methodological analysis of what the category
"religious authority" licenses scholars or participants to infer.

### CR-D: Canonicality as a functional status

Canonicity can carry memory, identity, circulation, legitimacy, and community
functions. Those functions are analytically distinct from proposition-specific truth
selection.

### CR-E: Socially recognizable authority

The W component in B** is not merely individual access to evidence. Religious
authority is socially mediated through recognized bearers, institutions, texts,
practices, and interpretive communities.

### CR-F: Etic/emic normativity boundary

The paper's truth-directed target is emic/internal to the structures being audited.
The scholar's task is second-order: determine what those structures establish and
what bridges are required. The paper does not adjudicate theology from outside.

## Higher-order synthesis

The religion-field scan raises the ceiling by changing the strongest defensible
religious-studies contribution from:

"religious authority is target-relative"

to a more disciplinary formulation:

Religious authority should be analyzed as a functionally differentiated knowledge
relation whose inferential contribution depends on its bearer, native function,
target, transfer rule, social recognizability, and relation to the proposition at
issue.

A second methodological result follows:

A fixed-target comparison can compare heterogeneous religious authority structures
without treating them as one natural kind, provided that native context is
reconstructed first and comparison is restricted to an explicit tertium.

## Journal Religion fit

The scan materially improves fit for Religion.

The journal currently:
- welcomes significant, novel, theoretical and methodological work in the academic
  study of religion;
- excludes straight theology and philosophy of religion;
- has recently published a thematic issue on the philosophy of religious studies;
- has recently published a thematic issue proposing a materialist model for
  comparing religion;
- has recently published a historical-sociological study of changing rabbinic
  authority.

The current paper can now be framed within those conversations without changing the
fixed case or spine.

Remaining risk:
If the manuscript is written primarily as "what gives a believer epistemic grounds
to believe Rashi or Ramban?", it will still read as philosophy of religion.

Higher-fit framing:
The article studies what different Jewish authority and knowledge structures
actually do when scholars or participants invoke them in relation to one fixed
truth-apt disagreement, and derives a comparative method for distinguishing
legitimacy, memory, institutional settlement, transmission, authentication, and
proposition-specific epistemic adjudication.

## Anti-bloat verdict

The twenty approaches do not become twenty literatures in the manuscript.

Likely article-visible clusters:
1. sociology of religious knowledge;
2. comparative method;
3. religious authority studies;
4. canon/tradition/memory;
5. normativity / philosophy of religious studies.

Supporting or footnote-level:
scripturalism, reception history, textual criticism, lived religion, material
religion, discourse/power.

Under the hood:
ritual reification, practice theory, category genealogy, and other controls unless a
reviewer or drafting dependency makes them load-bearing.

No change is required to:
- A;
- the fixed Rashi-Ramban case;
- Q as the third-party assessment problem;
- the source-module inventory logic;
- B**;
- the bounded result structure.

The ceiling was raised by changing the disciplinary significance and methodological
reach of C, not by adding a new spine node.
