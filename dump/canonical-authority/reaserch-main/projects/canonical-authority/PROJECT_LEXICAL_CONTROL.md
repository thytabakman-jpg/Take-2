# Canonical Authority — Lexical Control

> Controlled lexical layer for terms whose interpretation can change the project's result. This file is a semantic control surface, not a replacement for substantive project sources.

## PURPOSE

Prevent repeated ambiguity between ordinary project language and technical architectural language.

Rule:

When a term below is load-bearing, use its controlled meaning or explicitly mark the coordinate OPEN. Do not infer its meaning from hierarchy position, familiar wording, or current prose.

## H[F] CONTROL

When `H[F]` appears in the protected spine, read it as:

`H` = purposive, non-exhaustive admitted source-grounded response space.

`F` = the admission/coverage configuration that determines the current purposive scope
and organization of that space.

This interpretation is a working semantic control, not a claim that F is an
independent theoretical primitive. Exact formal encoding remains OPEN until the detailed
research architecture explicitly resolves it.

## ROAD CONTROL

Do not infer that two response families are naturally distinct merely because they have
separate labels. The substantive unit of comparison is the source-grounded profile and
its branch-level commitments.

Do not infer that every branch is a separate road. A branch earns separate treatment when
its premises, commitments, truth-selection relation, or historical attribution changes.

## CURRENT OPEN TERMS

## USAGE RULE

Do not introduce a synonym for a controlled term when the synonym changes the apparent type.

Do not compress multiple controlled terms into one phrase when doing so hides a material relation.

When a controlled term's coordinate is OPEN, preserve the OPEN state rather than choosing the most convenient reading.

## LEXICAL PD TEST

For any new load-bearing term ask:

1. What is its exact referent?
2. What type is it?
3. What relation does it express?
4. What scope/indexing conditions apply?
5. What alternative readings could change the result?
6. Which downstream objects depend on the distinction?
7. Is the term itself canonical, derived, or provisional?
