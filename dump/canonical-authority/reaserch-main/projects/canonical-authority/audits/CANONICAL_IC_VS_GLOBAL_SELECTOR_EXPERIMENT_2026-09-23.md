# Canonical Authority — ImprovementCore vs Global Selector Experiment

Date: 2026-09-23
Status: PROSPECTIVE CONTROLLED ANALYTIC EXPERIMENT
Mutation: none to Canonical authority objects; findings routed here for project review.

## Purpose
Compare, on frozen Canonical Authority tasks:
G = global selector without ImprovementCore;
I = ImprovementCore adaptive routing;
U = normalized union preserving legitimate independent challenges.

## Frozen tasks
T1 Corpus frontier (G1.2): decide the next analytic move for unresolved candidate-family
coverage/evidence closure without expanding the frozen theory.
T2 Publication readiness boundary (G1.5): determine whether "ready for rebuild" and
"manuscript absent/not yet drafted" create a planning/drafting admission inconsistency.
T3 Ceiling/preservation (G1.4/G1.3): seek a stronger contribution while preserving the
package-TRACE obstruction and theory freeze.
T4 Priority/control: select next meaningful Canonical work among G1.2 and G1.5 open
branches without unsupported scalar ranking.

freeze exact target/baseline/success condition; A01/A08/A11; A12/A13; A02/A04/A05;

Discovery:
The project-local production-admission method is not internal to IC. IC can diagnose the
boundary but does not itself provide the JHB-derived production-admission comparator.
Therefore global/local repertoire adds a genuine external capability.
Canonical state itself contains tension: STATE says manuscript_status absent_not_yet_drafted
while current_ceiling says manuscript_status ready_for_rebuild_from_v1_2_0 and G1.5 says
manuscript execution can proceed. This does not prove drafting authorization; it proves the
state uses at least two different readiness predicates that require explicit typing.
Result: U strictly more informative than I alone.

stronger-successor generation, not the project-specific non-total next-work ordering.
Normalized U:

Result: global/project-local selector is primary; IC is optional and can be over-selection.

## Cross-task comparison
IC strengths:
- mandatory HF-001 and frozen-frame discipline;
- sequencing discovery before generator selection;
- leverage/frontier localization;
- gain/non-regression/incomparability evaluation;
- recomputation and terminal A16.

IC limitations relative to global repertoire:
- does not subsume project-local production-admission comparators;
- does not replace project-specific priority selectors;
- external specialist methods can add evidence/comparator functions even when IC has a
  trigger route for related atomic capabilities.

Global selector strengths:
- can reach project-local and external methods outside IC;
- can select specialist comparators.
Global selector weakness:
- without containment normalization it redundantly selects IC children/composites.

## Main result
ImprovementCore is not choosing obviously bad tools on these four Canonical tasks.
Its adaptive routing is generally well matched when the job is actually improvement.
The larger failure is scope: the universal selector must know when IC is the engine,
when an external/project-local method supplements it, and when IC is unnecessary.

Observed classes:
T1 complementary / IC sequencing advantage
T2 normalized union strictly richer
T3 IC subsumes most global selections
T4 project-local selector primary; IC optional

## Canonical Authority findings routed from experiment
CA-F1: Publication readiness terminology is under-typed. "ready_for_rebuild",
"absent_not_yet_drafted", and "manuscript execution can proceed" are distinct predicates
and must not be treated as one readiness state.
CA-F2: G1.2 corpus work benefits from treating history reconstruction as conditional on
admission-identity sensitivity rather than mandatory for every evidence-closure move.
CA-F3: G1.3/G1.4 ceiling work is already strongly contained by IC; separately invoking
Raise-the-Ceiling and A16 with IC is normally redundant unless used as an independent
challenge.
CA-F4: Canonical's partial-order priority selector has a job IC does not replace:
non-total project-next-work ordering across open branches.

## Repertoire/selector architecture findings
R-F1 add engine-containment normalization before counting selection breadth.
R-F2 log engine actual child route.
R-F3 selection must distinguish ENGINE_PRIMARY, EXTERNAL_SUPPLEMENT,
INDEPENDENT_CHALLENGE, and PROJECT_LOCAL_PRIMARY.
R-F4 do not infer IC quality from selection frequency; compare frozen-task routing and delta.
