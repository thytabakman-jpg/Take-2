## Design rule

Sections implement the mature reader argument. They do not define the research architecture.

Each section has one dominant substantive job, and every section boundary must be licensed by a reader-state transition.

Historical rule retained:
The road map controls substantive jobs; section numbering and headings remain revisable.

## Candidate manuscript architecture

### Section 1 — Introduction: the fixed problem and the paper's contribution

Primary jobs:
- orient the reader to the factual disagreement;
- state the third-party adjudication question;
- identify the bounded contribution and literature boundary;
- preview the argument spine without performing the body argument in miniature.

Reader transition:
Unoriented reader -> knows exactly what disagreement and adjudication problem the paper addresses.

Units:
U1, U2, publication framing surrounding U12.

Contribution attachment:
Major contribution set preview only. No detailed contribution proof here.

Anti-bloat rule:
Do not prove the broader method or case result in the introduction.

### Section 2 — The Fixed Case and the Comparison Frame

Primary jobs:

Reader transition:
Knows the factual dispute -> understands the controlled comparison target.

Units:

### Section 3 — Roads, Native Functions, and Route-Specific Commitments

Primary job:
Show what the selected representative roads actually do in their native contexts and what substantive commitments come with granting each road.

Reader transition:
Understands the comparison target -> understands why the roads are genuinely different and why their commitments matter.

Unit:
U4.

### Section 5 — What the Current Case Actually Establishes

Primary jobs:

- apply the method to the current admitted corpus;
- identify the package-level TRACE obstruction;
- state the bounded fixed-case result.

Reader transition:
Knows the method and its possibility conditions -> knows what the actual evidence establishes and why the case stops.

Units:
U8, U9.

Major contribution attachment:
Bounded Rashi-Ramban result; target-relative authority implication.

Important placement rule:

### Section 6 — What Would Happen After a Successful Route?

Primary job:
State the conditional COMPOSE/defeat boundary.

Reader transition:
Knows the current case stops at TRACE -> understands why even a future successful route would not automatically settle a conflict among several successful grounds.

Unit:
U10.

Major contribution attachment:
Separate composition/defeat boundary.

Anti-bloat rule:
Use only enough COMPOSE machinery to establish the boundary unless the paper's general-characterization claim requires more.

### Section 7 — What This Shows About Religious Authority

Primary job:
Derive the broader target-relative and attribution-sensitive authority implication from the worked case and route architecture.

Reader transition:
Understands the case and general route -> understands the broader implication without treating "authority" as epistemically portable by status alone.

Unit:
U11.

Major contribution attachment:
Target-relative authority; licensed transfer; selector attribution; broader religious-studies implication.

Anti-bloat rule:
No detached claims about religion generally. Every broader claim must remain visibly derived from the worked case and method.

### Section 8 — Contribution, Scope, and Conclusion

Primary jobs:
- state the bounded contribution;
- state disciplinary significance at the defensible level;
- state literature-positioned novelty without exaggeration;
- state scope limits and reopen conditions;
- stop.

Reader transition:
Knows what the argument established -> knows exactly what the paper contributes and where its claims stop.

Unit:
U12.

Major contribution attachment:
All five major contribution claims, but presented as a dependency set rather than five flat claims.

## Historical continuity

The architecture preserves the strongest historical publication insight:

one destination
+
many roads
+
different substantive commitments
+
explicit road-to-destination movement
+
bounded stopping point.

It compresses the older section-per-response-family architecture into a representative-family body section while retaining the full road map in the research artifact.

## Comparison with historical publication architecture

The older v2.1/v2.4 models used more response-family-specific sections.

Current successor advantages:

- preserves the substantive jobs from the old architecture;
- makes the fixed target and adjudication question explicit before the roads;
- gives the road commitments a dedicated location;
- makes TRACE/DISCRIMINATE/RECOGNIZE visible as the method;
- prevents the positive control from being mistaken for the current result;
- keeps COMPOSE downstream and conditional;
- derives broader authority claims after the case rather than before it;
- leaves contribution/novelty as the earned endpoint;
- reduces section count without removing backend road coverage.

## Section-level failure tests

A section fails the architecture if:

- it performs two unrelated dominant epistemic jobs;
- it requires a concept whose first use occurs later;
- it assumes a conclusion earned only downstream;
- it narrates research chronology rather than reader dependency;
- it treats a diagnostic coordinate as an independent reader-facing theory;
- it makes the bounded case appear to traverse dormant downstream gates;
- it introduces a contribution before the argument has earned it.
