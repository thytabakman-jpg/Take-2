## Primary-source findings

Ramban Genesis 1:1 explicitly says:
- Maaseh Bereishit is a deep mystery not fully knowable from the verses;
- its complete knowledge comes by received tradition back to Moses;
- later, while directly disputing Rashi's construct/temporal peshat, Ramban gives what he calls the simple/correct explanation: bereshit is not functioning as Rashi's temporal construct and God initially created heavens/earth;
- he then separately introduces a "way of truth" / kabbalistic reading.

This confirms the existing project distinction:
received creation knowledge is present, and I_S is Ramban's peshat conclusion, but the text does not explicitly say "this syntactic/peshat conclusion I_S is itself the received item."

## Secondary-source finding with material relevance

David Shatz, "Is There Science in the Bible? An Assessment of Biblical Concordism," Tradition 41:2 (2008), pp. 226-227 and n. 90, argues that for Ramban Kabbalistic interpretation often is the peshat. He quotes David Berger's characterization that Nahmanides tends to equate peshat and sod by explaining the plain meaning through Kabbalistic doctrine. Shatz's n. 90 says Berger's published discussion adduces other Ramban passages (Gen 6:4, Exod 6:2-3, Num 20:1, Job 32:3) and reports Berger's statement to Shatz that Kabbalah likewise provides the peshat in Genesis 1. Shatz also reports Slifkin as affirming that the mystical meaning of Genesis 1 is, for Ramban, literal meaning.

This is materially closer to the missing bridge than the prior corpus.

However:
- Berger's cited published passage, by Shatz's own note, does not discuss Genesis 1.
- the Genesis 1 application is reported as a personal statement to Shatz.
- the proposition supported is broad: Kabbalah can provide/explain peshat in Genesis 1.
- it does not specifically establish that the exact syntactic proposition I_S is received content.
- it also does not yet supply a proposition-specific rule from the received tradition to the non-construct/main-predication reading I_S.

Therefore this evidence strengthens the plausibility of a CONTENT-TO-PESHAT bridge but does not close the CONTENT-TO-I_S edge.

CONTENT-TO-TARGET IMPLICATION: NOT CLOSED.

## Exact syntactic search

Targeted searches for scholarship connecting Ramban's rejection of Rashi's construct-state/temporal reading specifically to kabbalah/received tradition did not locate a source making that connection.

Accessible primary text instead presents Ramban's grammatical/peshat argument directly:
- he discusses Rashi's construct-state objection;
- adduces biblical counterexamples;
- then states the simple/correct reading with bereshit non-construct and bara as main predication.

That is first-order exegetical evidence for I_S, but under the current inquiry it is not second-order evidence that I_S is received from the privileged tradition.
