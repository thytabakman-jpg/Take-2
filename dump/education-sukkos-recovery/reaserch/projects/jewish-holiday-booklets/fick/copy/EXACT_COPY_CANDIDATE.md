WHAT HAPPENS WHEN CONCENTRATIONS DIFFER?

J = -D dC/dx

J
Net diffusive flux
The net amount moving through each unit of area over time.

D
Diffusion coefficient
How easily particles spread through this medium.

dC/dx
Concentration gradient
How quickly concentration changes across distance.

HIGHER CONCENTRATION

LOWER CONCENTRATION

Particles move randomly both ways.

With a concentration gradient, the net diffusive flux is toward lower concentration.

A difference can still be present while diffusion is happening.

In this simple setup, diffusion redistributes particles, so the concentration difference becomes smaller over time.

People are not particles. Physics does not tell us how relationships work.

WHEN DIFFERENCE FEELS LIKE DISTANCE

Some disagreements are easy to laugh about. Others can make people feel far apart.

“WAIT... YOU REALLY BELIEVE THAT?”

Sometimes the hardest part is what the disagreement makes you start thinking about the other person.

A JEWISH POSSIBILITY

Rav Kook argues that real peace does not require everyone to think the same way. Different sides can have a place within a larger whole.

Rav Kook
Olat Re’iyah
On Berakhot 64a

That is Rav Kook’s idea. The questions below are our classroom tool.

TRY FOUR QUESTIONS

1. WHAT IS ACTUALLY DIFFERENT?

Name the disagreement clearly.

2. WHAT IS STILL SHARED?

Name the relationship, goal, activity, or concern that remains.

3. WHAT DO WE ACTUALLY NEED TO COORDINATE?

What has to work together for the shared thing to happen?

4. WHAT CAN STAY DIFFERENT?

Not every difference needs one shared answer.

This tool does not tell us who is right. It helps us see what the shared situation actually requires.

SAME DISAGREEMENT. TWO SITUATIONS.

You like pineapple on pizza. Your friend does not.

Separate slices
No shared decision about pineapple.

One shared pizza
One decision about the pizza you share.

The disagreement stayed the same. What they were sharing changed.

One disagreement does not have to become your whole picture of the person.

HOW DO DIFFERENT PEOPLE BUILD ONE SUKKAH TOGETHER?

כל ישראל ראויין לישב בסוכה אחת

“All Israel are fit to sit in one sukkah.”

Sukkah 27b

All Israel? That’s a lot of different people.

The Gemara is discussing the mitzvah of sukkah. We are using its image of one shared sukkah for our classroom challenge.

BUILD ONE EDIBLE SUKKAH TOGETHER

FOR THIS CLASSROOM MODEL

1. Build one shared structure.

2. Give it walls, an opening, and a separate roof area to represent schach.

3. Use only your group’s materials.

4. Finish with one sukkah that the group built together.

This is a classroom model. It is not instructions for building a kosher sukkah.

BEFORE YOU BUILD

Each person chooses one design preference.

Your group has one set of materials and one shared structure.

DURING THE BUILD

DO WE NEED TO COORDINATE HERE?

CAN THIS STAY DIFFERENT?

AFTER YOU BUILD

WHAT STAYED DIFFERENT?

WHERE DID WE HAVE TO COORDINATE?

WHAT DID WE STILL BUILD TOGETHER?

WHERE ELSE DO WE NEED TO COORDINATE, AND WHERE CAN WE STAY DIFFERENT?

MAKE ROOM FOR EACH OTHER

TWO PEOPLE. TWO VIEWS. ONE CONVERSATION.

Choose a safe real or invented disagreement where you still share a relationship, group, plan, or activity.

Different disagreements can affect what people can do together in different ways.

FIRST, MAKE ROOM TO HEAR

Before you respond, say the other person’s view back to them.

Ask:

“Did I understand you?”

NOW FIND THE BOUNDARY

WHAT IS DIFFERENT?

WHAT ARE YOU STILL SHARING OR DOING TOGETHER?

WHAT DO YOU ACTUALLY NEED TO COORDINATE?

WHAT CAN STAY UNRESOLVED?

WHAT CAN YOU DO NEXT?

ON THE DISSOLVING PAPER

Write:

WE HAVE TO AGREE ABOUT EVERYTHING BEFORE WE CAN KEEP GOING.

Put the paper in the water.

Watch it dissolve.

THE DISAGREEMENT DID NOT DISAPPEAR.

THE DEMAND FOR TOTAL AGREEMENT DID.

What can you still do together while the disagreement remains?
