## Page job

Keep the existing Page 2 job:

turn the Page 1 insight into a usable method for inspecting whether a question fits what the learner is actually trying to find out.

## Student-state change

Before Page 2:
A question can reduce the live possibilities.

After Page 2:
I can inspect a question before answering it and improve its fit to the matter.

## Emotional mechanism

The emotional depth comes from a recognizable low-stakes interpersonal misfire.

The page lets the learner feel:

"That happens."
"The question itself can jump ahead."
"I can slow the problem down before the argument starts."

No personal disclosure is required.

Do not add therapy language or a separate emotional lesson.

## Is/ought boundary

Backend only:

Mathematics describes information structure.
It does not establish a norm for how people ought to ask.

Pirkei Avot supplies the Jewish inquiry habit:
שואל כענין

The student page does not need the phrase "is/ought."

Bridge line:

The source gives the habit.

Three-part model:

TARGET

What exactly am I trying to find out?

RELEVANT FACTS

What information matters to this?

ANSWER TEST

What would count as settling it?

Worked repair:

REPAIR THE FIRST DRAFT

Instead of starting with
"Why are you ignoring me?"

Start by finding the missing fact:

"Did you hear me when I called you?"

Practice strip:

TRY IT

A class is testing two paper bridges.

"Which bridge is better?"

Before answering, make "better" clear.

TARGET
What are we comparing?

RELEVANT FACTS
What measurements matter?

ANSWER TEST
What result would settle it?

Bottom declaration:

A strong question fits the matter before it asks for the answer.
