# A. Main-text candidates for the overfull draft

## A1. Correctness-Scope Trilemma

Recovered from the Mesorah project.

Three branches:

1. The tradition does not guarantee correctness.
   Result: membership/status cannot establish truth.

2. The tradition guarantees correctness and the disputed knowledge lies inside the guaranteed scope.
   Result: exactly one rival preserves the guaranteed content, but shared canonical status still does not identify which rival it is.

3. The tradition guarantees correctness but the disputed knowledge lies outside the guaranteed scope.
   Result: the tradition does not settle this factual question; another adjudicative basis is required.

Why it helps:
- sharpens H_M protected reception;
- converts vague "mesorah may mean different things" into an exhaustive local branching argument;
- gives the reader a memorable theorem-like result;
- reconnects the new architecture to the original Mesorah question without reverting to a Mesorah-only paper.

Best location:
Section 2.2 after protected reception, or Section 5 as a derived case-independent consequence.

Novelty posture:
paper-specific trilemma / synthesis; no historical-priority claim required.

## A2. Circularity refined as an asymmetry test

Recovered from the earlier authority/mesorah work and Bergmann comparator work.

Key refinement:

> Circular support is not automatically defective. The damaging case is reciprocal self-authentication that fails to produce a truth-relevant asymmetry between the rivals.

Authority-based proposal:
- an authority identifies the authentic tradition;
- that authority is authoritative because it embodies or transmits the authentic tradition.

Two outcomes:
- reciprocal loop supplies no rival-specific asymmetry -> no adjudication;
- authority has independently warranted truth-relevant asymmetry -> that independent feature is the actual discriminator and must be named.

Why it helps:
- avoids the crude claim "circularity bad";
- gives a stronger philosophical connection to epistemic circularity;
- integrates naturally with Selector Attribution and bridge licensing;
- protects against the obvious objection that some epistemic circularity can be benign.

Best location:
Section 3 after bridge licensing, or Section 5.2 after Selector Attribution.

## A3. Re'em / recension as explanation-versus-adjudication control

Recovered from the original Rashi-Ramban architecture.

Candidate:
Grant the variant-recension proposal as strongly as evidence allows.

Result:

- it changes transmission history / represented bearer;

Why it helps:
- answers a concrete objection to the fixed case;
- makes "explanation of disagreement ≠ adjudication of truth" vivid rather than abstract;
- supplies a source-specific example of the paper's provenance distinction.

Best location:
Section 1.1 or Section 2 as a short objection/control paragraph.

## A4. Yeridas Hadoros splits into two different propositions

Earlier work distinguished:

A. epistemic modesty / unknowability:
"we later observers cannot know which rival is correct."

B. epoch reliability:
"earlier generations are more reliable on this proposition type."

These are not the same claim.

A can license bounded suspension without identifying a winner.
B requires:
- a source-supported reliability relation;
- domain specification;
- case asymmetry between the rivals.

Why it helps:
- stronger PD example;
- prevents slogan-level analysis;
- enriches H_G beyond generic generational hierarchy;
- connects directly to positive suspension.

Best location:
Section 2.3.

## A5. Pluralism splits into legitimacy and expansive truth pluralism

Current manuscript discusses legitimacy, but older work distinguished:

1. pluralist legitimacy:
opposed views may remain religiously/canonically legitimate;

2. expansive pluralism:
contradictory propositions are both true in the same respect.

The first does not adjudicate descriptive truth.
The second is a much stronger logical/metaphysical thesis and needs independent defense.

Why it helps:
- answers a common eilu v'eilu / shivim panim response more directly;
- prevents equivocation between "both legitimate" and "both descriptively true";
- gives the paper a clear non-contradiction control without turning it into a theology fight.

Best location:
Section 2.2.

## A6. Functional requirements on any proposed discriminator

Recovered six-criterion framework, refined so classification quality is separate from truth tracking.

For a proposed rule/criterion asked to resolve factual disagreement, useful controls include:

- truth-relevant reliability;
- specificity;
- non-circularity;
- operationality;
- intensional adequacy;
- non-ad-hoc stability.

Important refinement:
classification reproducibility is not the same as truth-tracking reliability.

Why it helps:
- gives TRACE/bridge licensing a concrete operational face;
- supplies reviewer-visible standards for "licensed";
- lets the paper test a proposed "authentic mesorah" criterion rather than merely asking for one.

Best location:
short main-text paragraph in Section 3 plus fuller footnote/table.

Do not market this six-item list as a universal theory.

## A10. Inverse problem of religious outcomes

If a reviewer asks "what licenses the rule that licenses COMPOSE?", the answer is already developed:

Every outcome-material selector must be:
- source-attributable;
