# 1. The Disagreement, the Recurring Problem, and the Answer

## 1.1 The Exact Disagreement

<!-- P01 -->
Bereishis 1:1 presents a precise grammatical disagreement between Rashi and Ramban. Rashi's peshat reads the opening as dependent: *bereishis* stands in a construct/dependent relation with the verbal clause *bara Elohim*, so the opening supplies a temporal setting whose main clause arrives in verse 3. Rashi supports that reading with the broader claim that *reishis* in Scripture occurs in construct relation. Ramban directly attacks that grammatical premise, citing cases such as Yeshayahu 46:10 and Devarim 33:21 where *reishis* is not functioning as Rashi's generalization requires, and then gives an independent peshat reading of Bereishis 1:1. Let (C_S) denote the verse-level proposition that, on the peshat advanced, *bereishis* functions in the construct/dependent relation Rashi assigns to it, so the opening forms the temporal setting leading to the main clause in verse 3. Rashi's position is therefore (P_R = C_S), while Ramban's is (P_N = \neg C_S). Rashi's broader claim about *reishis* is a supporting grammatical premise; the fixed target is the verse-level construct/dependent reading itself.

## 1.2 A Recurring Problem and the Paper's Answer

<!-- P02 -->
The Rashi–Ramban collision instantiates a recurrent adjudicative form rather than an isolated anomaly. Truth-apt machlokes appears across canonical strata: R. Akiva and R. Yishmael disagree over particulars connected with Sinai and Ohel Moed; R. Yochanan and Reish Lakish disagree over the Flood and Eretz Yisrael; and Geonic authorities divide over the Endor episode. The subject matter changes, but the second-order problem recurs across Tannaim, Amoraim, Geonim, and Rishonim: later readers inherit incompatible claims from canonical authorities, while canonical standing alone does not identify which rival claim is true. This paper asks what source grounded basis, if any, can do that work. It argues that the relevant source material must be connected to the exact disputed proposition in a source-attributable way, the connection must favor one rival over the other, and a later evaluator must have warranted grounds for using it. Applied to the seven mechanism families examined here, the current evidence yields a bounded result: no independently admissible source package completes that route to (C_S). The grammatical verdict therefore remains open, while the analysis identifies exactly where the available second-order routes stop and what additional evidence would reopen the result.

## 1.3 The Comparison Frame

<!-- P03 -->
The comparison adopts a familiar move from comparative methodology: hold a common tertium comparationis fixed while allowing the comparands to remain different in their native character and function. Here the tertium is the disputed proposition pair (P_R) and (P_N). A halachic rule for handling disagreement, a doctrine about received content traced to Moshe, an institution with binding authority, a hierarchy of generations, and a procedure for authenticating prophecy can therefore enter the same comparison without being treated as versions of the same mechanism. The fixed proposition pair supplies the common question; the sources retain their different native jobs.

# 2. What the Sources Actually Do

## 2.1 Native Success and the Comparison Set

<!-- P04 -->
The source analysis begins from an established constraint: authority and expertise are domain-sensitive. Jewish sources can succeed at legitimating disagreement, determining practice, classifying provenance, authenticating a source, explaining historical development, or settling an institutional question. Each is a real result in its own domain. The present inquiry asks a narrower transfer question: what relation, if any, carries that native success to the truth of (C_S)? Native success and target success therefore remain separate throughout the comparison.

<!-- P05 -->
The paper tests seven mechanism families because together they span materially different ways a second-order Jewish structure might bear on the fixed question. Pluralism and determination test legitimacy and practical settlement; protected reception tests provenance; authority and error test institutional force under acknowledged fallibility; authenticated prophecy tests privileged epistemic access; Ramban's received creation tradition tests the route closest to the Bereishis case; loss and reconstruction test epistemic interruption and recovery; and generational hierarchy tests whether comparative stature can support reliability. The corpus is purposive rather than exhaustive. Its value lies in whether these functionally different mechanisms stop for the same reason or reveal different kinds of obstruction when pressed against one fixed truth target.

## 2.2 Pluralism and Received Tradition

<!-- P06 -->
Pluralism and determination reveal one obstruction type: legitimacy or practical settlement can succeed without yet selecting descriptive truth. Eruvin 13b preserves the famous formulation *eilu v'eilu divrei Elohim chayim* while also recording a practical determination in favor of Beis Hillel. The Ritva, reporting an answer attributed to the Rabbanei Tzarfat, develops one way of understanding how opposed positions can possess standing within the divine legal order. Modern scholarship on rabbinic pluralism has shown that the meaning and scope of such materials remain contested, which itself counsels against treating "elu ve elu" as a ready made theory of descriptive truth. For the present paper, the narrower point is enough. These sources concern legitimacy within disagreement and, in the Talmudic passage, practical determination. A rule that preserves legitimacy or determines practice establishes those native outputs. Truth assessment of the syntax of Bereishis 1:1 requires an additional relation connecting that native function to (C_S).

<!-- P07 -->
Protected reception reveals a second obstruction type: provenance can matter epistemically without yet establishing that the disputed proposition belongs to the protected content. Rambam distinguishes received material traced to Moshe from matters derived through interpretation and legal reasoning. In Hilchos Mamrim and in his Introduction to the Mishnah, provenance matters because different elements of Torah knowledge stand in different relations to reception and dispute. This can be epistemically significant. A proposition known to belong to a protected received category could possess an epistemic status unavailable to a later inference. Selection between (P_R) and (P_N) would then require grounds for placing the disputed syntactic proposition within that protected received content and for showing that the classification asymmetrically favors one rival. Protected reception can therefore classify the kind of knowledge at issue, or relocate a disagreement outside the protected category, without automatically adjudicating the proposition itself.

## 2.3 Authority, Loss, and Hierarchy

<!-- P08 -->
Authority and error reveal a third obstruction type: institutional force can remain genuine while correctness on the target proposition remains open. The Horayos tradition treats authoritative rulings and error within the same legal framework. Mishnah Horayos, the Bavli, and Rambam's codification can regulate what follows from an erroneous authoritative decision precisely because the framework allows authoritative decision and error to coexist. That structure matters for the present comparison. An institution can govern legal consequences or command compliance while still requiring a further argument before its authority becomes evidence that a descriptive proposition is true. Applied to the Bereishis case, institutional standing would need a truth directed relation to (C_S) and an asymmetry between the rival readings. That truth directed relation is what would allow institutional authority to bear on the present problem rather than only on its native legal output.

<!-- P09 -->
Loss and reconstruction, and generational hierarchy, expose two further obstruction types: epistemic history can explain how knowledge changes without supplying a present selector, while stature can create an asymmetry without supplying a proposition-type reliability bridge. Temurah 16a describes Torah material forgotten after Moshe's death and a process of recovery through reasoning. That narrative can explain how transmission is interrupted and how knowledge can later be reconstructed. A rule for choosing between Rashi and Ramban would require an additional proposition specific basis. Likewise, statements in Shabbos 112b and Eruvin 53a express asymmetries of generational stature, memory, or intellectual capacity. Such asymmetries can matter while leaving open whether earlier status tracks accuracy across every proposition type. Later halachic sources can even recognize forms of later informational advantage. To use generational hierarchy here, the paper would need a source grounded relation between the relevant kind of stature and reliability on Biblical Hebrew syntax, together with a case specific asymmetry between the two commentators.

## 2.4 Prophecy and Ramban's Creation Tradition

<!-- P10 -->
Authenticated prophecy supplies the positive contrast: this kind of traditional mechanism can in principle reach descriptive truth when privileged access actually covers the target and is warrantedly recognizable. In Hilchos Yesodei HaTorah, Rambam both constrains the legal role of prophecy and sets out conditions by which a prophet is authenticated. The mechanism concerns a purported channel of knowledge whose credibility can, under the right conditions, be assessed, giving it a function beyond social status. This makes prophecy an important positive control for the paper's method. An authenticated prophet whose revelation actually covered (C_S), selected one of the rival propositions, and was recognizable to the evaluator through warranted authentication would provide the kind of truth directed route the analysis is looking for. The current case supplies no instance of those conditions. The example instead establishes a structural point: traditional religious mechanisms can satisfy the architecture when the required relations are present.

<!-- P11 -->
Ramban's own discussion of creation exposes the most case-proximate obstruction: topic and commentator proximity can be extremely strong while proposition coverage remains unestablished. In his comment on Bereishis 1:1, he explicitly treats the process of creation as a profound matter known fully through tradition received from Moshe. Yet the same comment also contains a separately articulated peshat discussion and later introduces an esoteric "way of truth." The current source record therefore establishes that received creation knowledge and the interpretation of Bereishis 1 coexist within Ramban's treatment. Proposition coverage requires a further step: evidence that Ramban's rejection of Rashi's construct/dependent reading (\neg C_S) is itself among the received esoteric contents or is truth-supported by them. Topic proximity alone supplies weaker support. A tradition can concern creation without containing every exegetical judgment made in the same discussion. The strongest case proximate route therefore reaches the right topic and the right commentator, but the proposition specific edge remains unestablished.

## 2.5 The Pattern Across the Sources

<!-- P12 -->
The comparison therefore yields an early paper-specific result: heterogeneous authorities can fail to adjudicate the same proposition for heterogeneous reasons. In this corpus, the stopping points include legitimacy without truth selection, provenance without demonstrated target membership, institutional force compatible with represented error, epistemic history without a present selector, stature without a reliability bridge, privileged access without actual target coverage, and case proximity without proposition coverage. That profile also fixes the boundary between source reconstruction and project-level application. A source can supply a bearer, native domain, native function, provenance claim, authority relation, or authentication structure; the analysis then asks whether that material supports a bridge to (C_S). Information introduced only by the bridge cannot be credited retroactively to the source. The same attribution rule governs later source combination. The next question is therefore precise: what has to happen before any reconstructed source material becomes a ground for preferring (P_R) or (P_N)?

# 3. From Source to Ground

## 3.1 TRACE

<!-- P13 -->
The paper's proposed methodological contribution begins here. The individual ideas involved—source relevance, domain-sensitive authority, evidential support, and warranted uptake—have substantial precedents; the claim developed in this section concerns how they must be separated and connected when heterogeneous religious mechanisms are tested against one fixed truth target. The first job is TRACE. PROFILE names the prior setup work already performed in the source reconstructions: what the source is, what it natively does, and which commitments travel with it. TRACE then asks whether an independently admissible source package supplies a licensed and source-attributable relation from that native material to the fixed target. A source can enter TRACE in its own conceptual vocabulary and historical setting. The application succeeds only when the relation to the Rashi Ramban dispute can be reconstructed without inserting the very proposition-specific force that the application is meant to demonstrate.

<!-- P14 -->
TRACE must be package aware. Corpus admission asks why a mechanism belongs in the comparison at all; package admissibility asks whether two or more already admitted sources may legitimately be used together to establish one target-indexed ground. Individually incomplete sources can sometimes become jointly informative, so singleton failure cannot be promoted directly to corpus failure. TRACE therefore ranges over independently licensed source packages, with a one-source package as the singleton special case. Joint use still requires a principled basis for combining the sources and a demonstration that their combined content completes the target relation.

<!-- P15 -->
This creates the first of two distinct multiplicity problems. Package TRACE asks whether many sources can jointly establish one ground:

\[
\text{many sources} \rightarrow \text{one ground}.
\]

COMPOSE, introduced below, asks what follows when many already completed grounds bear on one case:

\[
\text{many grounds} \rightarrow \text{one verdict}.
\]

Keeping those operations separate prevents source combination from silently importing the selector needed for case-level adjudication.

## 3.2 DISCRIMINATE

<!-- P16 -->
Target relevance is the beginning of discrimination rather than its completion. A route can reach (C_S) while leaving both rivals equally situated. DISCRIMINATE asks whether the traced relation is asymmetric in the fixed case. Does it support (P_R) over (P_N), or (P_N) over (P_R), concerning truth? This requirement prevents topic relevance from masquerading as adjudication. It also compresses several lower level diagnostics into one reader facing job. Proposition linkage, case instantiation, and truth relevance matter because together they must yield a rival specific difference. When they do not, the route may illuminate the dispute while still leaving the evaluator without a reason to choose between the competing claims.

## 3.3 RECOGNIZE

<!-- P17 -->
A genuine source side discriminator becomes evaluator usable only through an additional step. RECOGNIZE marks that step. The evaluator needs warranted grounds for identifying the relevant source relation and relying on it in the case at hand. Contemporary work on expertise and epistemic authority helps clarify why source side support and evaluator uptake are distinct. A source can possess an epistemically important relation that a third party cannot reliably identify, authenticate, or use. In religious contexts, texts, institutions, interpretive communities, and recognized bearers can contribute to that uptake insofar as they participate in warranted access to the discriminator. Social recognition alone remains insufficient. The route level success condition is therefore layered. TRACE and DISCRIMINATE establish a source side ground; RECOGNIZE concerns its evaluator specific usability.

## 3.4 The COMPOSE Boundary

<!-- P18 -->
A further problem arises only when several completed grounds exist and materially conflict. At that point, an all things considered case result requires a further relation among the completed routes. COMPOSE names the separately licensed rules by which completed grounds can be aggregated, defeated, ranked, tied, or left unresolved. This must remain distinct from package TRACE. Package TRACE allows several sources to work together to establish one ground. COMPOSE operates only after several grounds already exist. The distinction also exposes a provenance issue. If the source side grounds are held fixed while a different composition rule changes the verdict, the selecting information is being introduced by the composition layer. A warranted conclusion can therefore be source only, mixed provenance, rule relative, tied, or unresolved. In the present Rashi Ramban case this downstream machinery remains conditional because the analysis never reaches multiple completed conflicting grounds.

# 4. Testing the Method

## 4.1 Positive Control

<!-- P19 -->
The prophetic route shows that the framework leaves room for religious authority to carry genuine epistemic relevance. Imagine an authenticated prophetic source whose revelation explicitly covered the syntactic proposition (C_S), whose content favored one of the rival readings, and whose prophetic status and target coverage were warrantedly recognizable to the evaluator. That route would TRACE to the proposition, DISCRIMINATE between the rivals, and satisfy the evaluator side condition for use. The example is deliberately conditional. Rambam's account supplies the structural model rather than an actual revelation concerning Bereishis 1:1. Its function in the argument is to demonstrate non vacuity. The method can register traditional privileged access as genuine truth directed force when the source itself supplies the required relations.

## 4.2 The Strongest Case-Proximate Route

<!-- P20 -->
The actual case is harder because Ramban's creation tradition gets close without completing the decisive edge. Ramban unquestionably invokes received knowledge concerning creation. That fact gives the route a stronger initial claim to relevance than generic appeals to authority or stature. The target is (C_S), the verse-level construct/dependent proposition on which Rashi and Ramban divide. Ramban's peshat is (\neg C_S). General relevance to creation therefore reaches only the topic, not yet the proposition. To complete TRACE through Ramban's received tradition, one would need evidence that the received creation knowledge contains, entails, or otherwise truth-supports Ramban's rejection of Rashi's construct/dependent reading. The recovered source record presently leaves that link open.

<!-- P21 -->
The strongest retrieved scholarship sharpens the problem rather than closing it. David Berger, discussing Ramban's relation between peshat and Kabbalah, reports Amos Funkenstein's argument that Bereishis 1:1 can display a syntactic divergence between the peshat and the "way of truth," including a different grammatical role for *Elohim*. David Shatz likewise reports scholarship that treats Ramban's Kabbalah as deeply involved in his reading of Bereishis 1. That material does not establish that Ramban's rejection of Rashi's construct/dependent reading (\neg C_S) is itself received content. The esoteric subject/object issue is a different syntactic coordinate and cannot simply be projected onto (C_S). Funkenstein's full text and the relevant portions of Moshe Halbertal's study remain open acquisition points in the current research record. The result must therefore remain calibrated to what has actually been inspected.

## 4.3 Where the Case Stops

<!-- P22 -->
Once source packages are tested rather than only singleton routes, the current bounded result survives. The package proof is compact. Any admissible package that excludes Ramban's received creation-tradition route lacks the only currently reconstructed route with direct case-proximate received-creation content. Any admissible package that includes that route inherits its unresolved proposition-specific edge, because none of the other admitted mechanisms supplies the missing connection from received creation knowledge to Ramban's rejection of the construct/dependent reading (\neg C_S). The current package space therefore divides into two classes, and neither completes TRACE to (C_S). Some routes still establish legitimacy, provenance, institutional force, epistemic history, hierarchy, or authenticated privileged access in principle, but the realized case stops before a source-attributable ground reaches the exact syntactic proposition. Because DISCRIMINATE, RECOGNIZE, and COMPOSE lie downstream of that obstruction, changing evaluator assumptions or composition rules cannot alter the fixed-case result while the TRACE gap remains. This conclusion remains bounded by the present corpus and evidence set and can be reopened by additional sources or newly recovered evidence.

# 5. Authority Across Targets

## 5.1 The Cross-Function Transfer Principle

<!-- P23 -->
The comparison yields a Cross-Function Transfer Principle. When a mechanism has authority with respect to one native output, its force bears on a different truth target only through an explicit relation connecting that native output to the new proposition. The source modules examined here succeed at legitimating disagreement, determining practice, classifying provenance, governing institutional consequences, describing epistemic loss or hierarchy, or authenticating privileged access. Their force in those domains does not by itself specify how that force bears on (C_S). The paper's contribution is therefore narrower than generic domain sensitivity: cross-function transfer requires an inspectable relation that carries epistemic force to the new target.

## 5.2 The Selector Attribution Principle

<!-- P24 -->
The same analysis yields a Selector Attribution Principle. Hold the source content fixed and vary a materially outcome-relevant bridge or composition rule. If the verdict changes, the difference is attributable at least partly to that added layer rather than to the fixed source alone. Mixed provenance can be entirely legitimate when its components are explicit: a conclusion may arise from source content plus a licensed inferential rule, or from source content as usable by an evaluator under specified warrants. What the principle blocks is silent transfer, where selector information supplied by an analyst, community, bridge, or meta rule is redescribed as though it had always been contained in the underlying source.

## 5.3 What the Scholar Can Claim

<!-- P25 -->
This division of labor also locates the paper's disciplinary claim. The scholar can analyze which internal Jewish structures are presented as capable of bearing on truth, what those structures natively establish, and what additional relations are required before they can adjudicate this dispute while leaving the ultimate Rashi Ramban verdict open. The first order target remains emic in the important sense that the rival claims concern what is true. The analysis is etic in the sense that it reconstructs and compares the inferential roles of the mechanisms without demanding assent to their theological premises. That combination permits rigorous truth directed analysis in religious studies without turning the article into a theological verdict. It also gives the paper a way to distinguish empirical source reconstruction, conceptual transfer analysis, and the final bounded case result rather than treating them as one evidential layer.

# 6. What the Analysis Establishes

## 6.1 The Contribution

<!-- P26 -->
The contribution is best understood as a dependency package built on established comparative and epistemic-authority insights. Fixed-target comparison and domain-sensitive authority supply the starting conditions. The paper then develops a source-to-ground route as PROFILE, TRACE, DISCRIMINATE, and RECOGNIZE, with TRACE capable of operating over independently admissible source packages; separates package TRACE from the later COMPOSE problem that arises only when several completed grounds conflict; and requires outcome-material selector information to remain attributed to the layer that supplies it. It then applies that architecture to the Rashi Ramban case and obtains a bounded package-level TRACE obstruction, protected by a positive control showing that success is possible in principle. The broader payoff is therefore an attribution-sensitive account of cross-function authority transfer, rather than a claim to have discovered domain sensitivity itself.

## 6.2 What Is Inherited and What Is New

<!-- P27 -->
The novelty claim has three layers. The paper inherits fixed-target comparison, domain-sensitive authority, source relevance, evaluator recognition, multi-premise support, and reason aggregation from existing comparative, epistemological, and argumentation literatures. It develops their integration here into a source-attributable route architecture with package-aware TRACE, rival-specific DISCRIMINATE, evaluator-relative RECOGNIZE, a separate COMPOSE boundary, and explicit selector attribution. It then obtains a bounded worked result in the Rashi–Ramban case: no currently admissible source package completes TRACE to (C_S), and that obstruction makes downstream evaluator and composition variation irrelevant while it persists. No direct predecessor for that integrated architecture and worked result has been located in the comparator corpus reviewed for this project.

## 6.3 The Bounded Answer

<!-- P28 -->
The paper can now give a precise answer to the question with which it began. Across the currently admitted and audited second order structures, every recovered source package stops short of a completed, proposition specific, third party usable ground for selecting between Rashi's (P_R) and Ramban's (P_N). The conclusion remains bounded to this corpus and evidence set. The corpus is purposive rather than exhaustive, several publication facing source tasks remain open, and new evidence could reopen the strongest case proximate route. What the method adds is a way to state exactly what would have to change. A future source or source package would need to supply a licensed relation to (C_S), generate a rival specific asymmetry, and be warrantedly usable by the relevant evaluator. Canonical standing, native authority, provenance, explanation, and topic proximity each contribute only the work their source grounded relations support. A proposition specific edge would reopen the result. The present boundedness makes the conclusion recoverable, falsifiable, and proportionate to the evidence.
