## Global prose rule

State the affirmative claim first. Use negation only when the distinction itself requires it. Avoid the recurring pattern “not X; rather Y,” including softer variants such as “the question is not X; it is Y,” “the point is not X; it is Y,” and “this does not mean X; it means Y.” Reader-facing prose begins from what the paper affirms, then marks exclusions only when they carry real argumentative work.

Use **Bereishis (Genesis) 1:1** on first reader-facing occurrence and **Bereishis** thereafter. Formal bibliographic source citations may retain **Genesis** when that is the title used by the edition or citation standard.

## Local 36 rule

For every paragraph-level question, run the directed six-scope surface

[
S\times S,qquad
S=\{SYS,SUB,COMP,INT,BND,XL\}.
]

The scopes are:

- **SYS** — the paragraph as a whole and its paper-level job.
- **SUB** — its internal semantic units and relation to the surrounding section.
- **COMP** — individual claims, sentences, terms, and modifiers.
- **INT** — reader handoff, syntax, sequence, cadence, and next-question pull.
- **BND** — conceptual ownership and distinctions that cannot be collapsed.
- **XL** — research-to-prose, evidence-to-claim, manuscript-to-field transfer.

All 36 directed cells are checked. The records below preserve only cells that create a live obligation, counterexample, or bridge. Unlisted cells passed without creating an additional drafting obligation.

Promotion rule: a locally stronger sentence is rejected when it weakens source fidelity, claim calibration, reader state, section job, boundedness, or the paper-level argument.

## First-draft weakness pass

After the companion manuscript is complete, score weakness by evidence and reader failure, not by stylistic preference alone.

Reopen a paragraph when one or more of these occurs:

1. the reader cannot state what changed after reading it;
2. the paragraph earns more than its evidence supports;
3. source material is doing work that belongs to project-level inference;
4. a conceptual boundary is blurred;
5. the next paragraph depends on an inference not yet earned;
6. the paragraph contains backend machinery with no reader-facing payoff;
7. a stronger formulation survives the local 36 without damaging boundedness or attribution.

The first redraft pass targets the weakest surviving paragraphs rather than globally rewriting already-working prose.
