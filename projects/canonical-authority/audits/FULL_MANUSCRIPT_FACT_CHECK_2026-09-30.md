## Scope

This audit checks the entire manuscript for externally verifiable factual claims, historical dates, primary-source attributions, secondary-scholarship attributions, and claims about the project's own research process.

It does **not** treat the paper's own formal definitions, philosophical deductions, or methodological proposals as external facts. Those are checked here only for whether they accurately describe the cited source material and whether an external citation is needed.

The manuscript was **not edited** during this fact-check run.

## Disposition key

- **GREEN — VERIFIED:** direct primary source or strong scholarly/reference support located.
- **GREEN/ANALYTIC:** source premise verified; further sentence is explicitly the paper's inference.
- **YELLOW — SOURCE/CALIBRATION:** plausible or substantially supported, but publication wording or citation needs tightening.
- **RED — CORRECT:** current manuscript statement should be changed.
- **OPEN — PRIMARY CLOSURE:** not contradicted, but exact primary support remains unclosed.

# Actual corrections / high-priority changes

## RED 1 — R. Yishmael's date is too precise and probably wrong as written

Current manuscript:

> R. Yishmael (90–135 CE)

A substantial reference source gives Rabbi Ishmael ben Elisha as approximately **c. 50–c. 135 CE**. Other references are less precise about his birth. The current “90” birth year is not adequately supported.

Recommended manuscript form:

> R. Yishmael (c. 50–c. 135 CE)

or, if the paper prefers maximum chronological caution, use a numeric **fl.** range supported by the selected reference.

This is the one item in the current date set that should be treated as a factual correction rather than merely a citation preference.

## YELLOW 2 — Ritva's life dates are genuinely uncertain

Current manuscript:

> Ritva (c. 1250–1330)

Reference sources conflict. A peer-reviewed Bar-Ilan record uses 1250–1320. Other reference literature places his birth around 1260 and death somewhere from 1314 to 1328. Some popular reference works use 1250–1330.

The attribution of the Eruvin comment to Ritva is verified; the life-date precision is not.

Recommended approach under the project's numeric-year rule:

> Ritva (fl. c. 1280–1320)

if an activity range is acceptable, or retain a life-date estimate only with the exact biographical source cited.

## YELLOW 3 — Eliyahu Mizrahi's date is approximate

Current manuscript:

> Eliyahu Mizrahi (c. 1450–1526)

Jewish Encyclopedia gives approximately 1455 and death in 1525 or 1526. The current range is close and not argument-relevant, but it should be tied to a chosen authority.

This is calibration, not a substantive paper error.

## YELLOW 4 — Judah Halevi's birth date is conventional, not settled

Current manuscript:

> Judah Halevi (c. 1075–1141)

Older Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy versions use c. 1075–1141. The 2026 replacement entry states that the birth year is unknown and circumstantial evidence only places it within a broader numeric window.

The current date is a conventional estimate and can be retained with citation. It should not be treated as a settled precise biography.

# Section-by-section source audit

## 1.1 — Rashi, Ramban, and Re'em

### GREEN — Rashi's grammatical reading

Direct Rashi on Genesis 1:1 supports all material points used by the manuscript:

- the verse is not presenting heaven and earth as the first chronological act;
- *reishis* is said to occur in construct relation;
- *bereishis bara* is explained like “at the beginning of God's creating”;
- the narrative reaches its operative main clause with God's command for light;
- Isaiah 46:10 is treated through ellipsis (“from the beginning [of a thing]”).

The manuscript's distinction between Rashi's broad *reishis* generalization and the narrower fixed verse-level target is accurate.

### GREEN — Ramban's attack

Direct Ramban on Genesis 1:1 explicitly raises:

- Isaiah 46:10, where he says *reishis* is not construct;
- Deuteronomy 33:21, likewise used as a counterexample;
- the response that if a missing noun can be supplied there, it can also be supplied in Genesis 1:1;
- his own “correct and clear” peshat beginning from creation ex nihilo.

The manuscript accurately states the logical consequence: a genuine counterexample defeats Rashi's stated universal support **as stated** without itself proving every part of Ramban's reading.

### GREEN — Re'em variant-recension proposal

This was previously an OPEN source obligation and is now directly verified.

Mizrahi on Genesis 1:1 states, in substance, that it appears Ramban's version of Rashi contained the comment only through the Hosea example and then proceeded directly to the later objections. That is exactly the textual-history proposal the manuscript attributes to Re'em.

**Publication consequence:** the Re'em item can be removed from the source-closure OPEN list once the exact edition/reference is inserted into the citation apparatus.

## 2.2 — Eruvin, Ritva, Rambam, and Halevi

### GREEN — Eruvin 13b

Eruvin 13b directly contains both:

- *eilu v'eilu divrei Elohim chayim*;
- practical determination in favor of Beit Hillel.

The manuscript accurately distinguishes standing/legitimacy from practical selection as two source-side outputs.

### GREEN — Ritva / Rabbanei Tzarfat attribution

Direct Ritva on Eruvin 13b explicitly says the “Rabbanei Tzarfat” asked how opposed rulings can both be words of the living God and reports their answer involving forty-nine faces for prohibition and forty-nine for permission, with determination entrusted to the sages.

The attribution is verified.

The manuscript's broader epistemic interpretation is an analytic use of that source, not a quotation of Ritva's own philosophical vocabulary.

### GREEN — Rambam protected-reception distinction

Mishneh Torah, Hilchot Mamrim 1:3 directly states that matters received from Moses do not admit genuine dispute and that the existence of dispute indicates the matter was not received from Moses in that protected sense. It separately discusses matters derived through reasoning and majority decision.

The manuscript's “received-content model” is source-grounded.

The correctness-scope trilemma is the paper's own analytic extrapolation and is correctly presented as such.

### GREEN — Judah Halevi transmission model

Kuzari III:24 directly appeals to the evidential force of a multitude transmitting Torah, branches, and interpretations across generations and argues against mass collusion.

The manuscript is justified in treating this as a distinct transmission-based epistemic model rather than merely as provenance classification.

The hypothetical application to (P_R) or (P_N) is the paper's own conditional test; the manuscript correctly says no qualifying history for either rival has been established.

## 2.3 — Authority, error, loss, hierarchy

### GREEN — Horayot

Horayot 2a explicitly treats erroneous court rulings and legal consequences for people acting in reliance on them.

The manuscript is source-grounded in saying authoritative legal decision and error can coexist in the native legal framework.

The further distinction among bindingness, settlement, and descriptive truth support is the paper's analysis.

### GREEN — epistemic circularity literature exists exactly as described

Michael Bergmann's published article is literally titled *Epistemic Circularity: Malignant and Benign* and argues that epistemic circularity is not uniformly fatal.

The sentence saying that literature distinguishes benign from malignant circularity is factually correct.

This item still needs the actual citation in a publication draft.

### GREEN — Temurah 16a loss/reconstruction

Temurah 16a reports forgotten material after Moses' death and says Othniel restored material through *pilpul* / reasoning.

The manuscript's historical/source premise is verified.

The “non-transmission principle” drawn from it is the paper's inference and should remain labeled as an analytic consequence rather than an explicit maxim stated by the Gemara.

### GREEN — Shabbat 112b / Eruvin 53a hierarchy

Shabbat 112b uses the early-generation/late-generation angel-human-donkey hierarchy. Eruvin 53a contrasts the intellectual capacity of earlier sages, later sages, and the speaker's generation.

The manuscript accurately says these texts express asymmetries of stature/intellectual capacity.

### YELLOW — *yeridas hadoros* as a developed doctrine

The paper's distinction between:
- epistemic modesty;
- comparative proposition-type reliability

is analytically useful and not contradicted by the sources checked.

However, if the article presents either branch as an established historical doctrine rather than a logical reconstruction of possible uses, it still needs the exact Beit Yosef / later-source apparatus already listed in the publication checklist.

## 2.4 — prophecy, Ruach HaKodesh, creation tradition

### GREEN — Rambam and prophetic authentication

Mishneh Torah, Hilchot Yesodei HaTorah chs. 8–9 supports both sides of the manuscript's description:

- an epistemic/legal procedure for recognizing prophets;
- strict limits on using prophecy to alter or decide Torah law.

The paper's authenticated-prophet (C_S) example is explicitly hypothetical, which is important. It is an analytic positive control, not a report that Rambam discusses Genesis 1:1.

### GREEN — Ramban Bava Batra 12a

Ramban's Talmudic novellae on Bava Batra 12a explicitly interpret “prophecy of sages” as knowledge of truth through *ruach hakodesh* within them.

The manuscript's claim that Ramban assigns truth-directed epistemic significance to an inspired form of wisdom is source-grounded.

### OPEN/YELLOW — Chatam Sofer

Secondary material confirms a Chatam Sofer tradition paralleling the distinction between ordinary prophecy and wisdom-assisted access to truth.

This audit did **not** close the exact primary Chatam Sofer passage and edition.

The manuscript's wording is plausible, but this remains a publication-facing source-closure item.

### GREEN — Ramban's received creation knowledge

Ramban on Genesis 1:1 directly states that creation is a deep mystery that cannot truly be known from the verses and is known through received tradition reaching Moses from God.

The manuscript's statement that received creation knowledge is explicitly present in the same comment is fully verified.

The further proposition-specific question—whether that received content includes or truth-supports Ramban's (
eg C_S) syntactic judgment—remains genuinely unestablished. The manuscript is correctly cautious here.

## 4.2 — Berger, Funkenstein, Shatz, Halbertal

### GREEN with contextual clarification — Berger reporting Funkenstein

The initial suspicion that this claim was too specific was wrong.

Berger's *Miracles and the Natural Order in Nahmanides* directly quotes Funkenstein's claim that in Genesis 1:1 the divergence reaches a syntactic contradiction and that, on the “way of genuine truth,” “God” is object rather than subject.

The manuscript is factually correct to say Berger reports this argument.

### YELLOW — add Berger's argumentative context

Berger cites Funkenstein **in order to challenge Funkenstein's broader thesis** that peshat and sod generally diverge. Berger argues instead that Ramban often tends to align peshat and sod.

Current wording:

> Berger ... reports Funkenstein's argument...

is literally true. Publication-quality wording should make the adversarial context visible so the reader does not mistake Berger for endorsing Funkenstein's general position.

### GREEN — Shatz

David Shatz's article explicitly cites Berger and reports that Berger told him Kabbalah also provides the peshat in Genesis 1. This supports the manuscript's statement that Shatz reports scholarship treating Kabbalah as deeply involved in Ramban's Genesis 1 reading.

### GREEN/OPEN — Halbertal

Moshe Halbertal's *Nahmanides: Law and Mysticism* is verified, including chapters on:
- the chain of being/creation;
- revelation and prophecy;
- esotericism and tradition.

The publisher's chapter descriptions explicitly connect his account to the first lines of Ramban's Genesis commentary.

The manuscript itself correctly says the exact relevant portions remain an OPEN acquisition point. The audit does not convert Halbertal into evidence that (
eg C_S) was received tradition.

### GREEN — Funkenstein bibliographical existence and dates

Amos Funkenstein's 1937–1995 dates are independently corroborated by Oxford and the University of California, Berkeley.
