## Raise-the-Ceiling rule used

A move survives only when it passes all five tests:

- **P — Preservation:** preserves paper identity, fixed case, and dependency spine.
- **A — Attachment:** attaches to a precise existing claim, section, or dependency.
- **U — Upward contribution:** raises significance, novelty, explanatory power, robustness, interdisciplinarity, or reader value.
- **D — Defensibility:** does not outrun the evidence.
- **N — Net gain:** scholarly gain exceeds added complexity, scope drift, reviewer attack surface, research burden, and word cost.

The recovered historical register still contains the superseded `I_S` target in places. This run uses the corrected live target `C_S`.

# Section 3 — What the Sources Actually Do

## Current job

Reconstruct the seven mechanisms in native context before transfer.

## Hidden underused result

The section currently reads partly as a source catalogue, but the sources generate a stronger comparative finding:

> The mechanisms do not fail for one generic reason. They stop at different inferential locations.

Current obstruction types:

1. **Pluralism/determination:** legitimacy or practical settlement without truth discrimination.
2. **Protected reception:** provenance significance without demonstrated target membership/coverage.
3. **Authority/error:** institutional force compatible with represented error.
4. **Loss/reconstruction:** epistemic history without a present proposition selector.
5. **Generational hierarchy:** status asymmetry without a proposition-type reliability bridge.
6. **Authenticated prophecy:** a structurally viable truth route without actual target coverage in this case.

This is more than “what each source says.” It is a **typed obstruction profile**.

Rebuild the section's reader-facing payoff around that profile.

> Heterogeneous authorities fail to adjudicate the same proposition in heterogeneous ways.

That gives the reader a paper-generated synthesis before Section 4.

# Section 6 — Authority Across Targets

## Current job

Derive the broader significance.

## Ceiling diagnosis

The section contains two strong paper-specific ideas but presents them as continuous prose:

1. cross-function authority transfer;
2. selector attribution.

Generic domain sensitivity is established background. The stronger contribution lies in combining transfer with inferential provenance.

## Ceiling candidate

Name two bounded principles:

### Cross-Function Transfer Principle

> Authority with respect to one native output bears on a different truth target only through an explicit relation connecting that native output to the new target.

### Selector Attribution Principle

> Holding source content fixed, any verdict difference produced by changing a materially outcome-relevant bridge or composition rule is attributable at least partly to that added layer rather than to the source alone.

These names make the actual contribution portable and citable without claiming that domain sensitivity itself is new.
